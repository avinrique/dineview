## 1. Non-dilutive and early funding in Karnataka / India (ELEVATE, SISFS, DPIIT, Karnataka policy, incubators, NASSCOM, cloud credits)

### Takeaway
As of late Oct 2026, the big non-dilutive options for this year have already closed. Karnataka ELEVATE's 25th call (up to ₹50 lakh grant, no equity) opened 25 May 2026 and closed in June/July 2026. SISFS stopped taking startup applications on 31 May 2026. The Google for Startups Accelerator India 2026 cohort was picked in July 2026. So DineView's realistic near-term moves are: get DPIIT recognition now (free, unlocks 80-IAC, the angel-tax exemption and scheme eligibility), get ready for the next ELEVATE call (likely mid-2027, inferred from the pattern), and stack cloud credits (Microsoft up to $5K self-serve, AWS Activate, bigger tiers through an incubator or VC referral).

### Cited Findings

**Karnataka ELEVATE (Idea2PoC grant-in-aid)**
- ELEVATE is the Karnataka government's flagship grant-in-aid programme. It gives a one-time grant of up to ₹50 lakh at the prototype/proof-of-concept stage. The 25th call opened on 25 May 2026, run by the Dept of Electronics, IT & Biotechnology — [CIOL, 23 May 2026](https://www.ciol.com/startups/karnataka-scales-elevate-to-back-more-early-stage-startups-11870143)
- In 2026, for the first time, all four tracks ran at once: ELEVATE General (all sectors), Shakti (≥51% women-owned), Unnati (SC/ST founders) and Aspire (Tier-2/3, beyond Bengaluru) — [CIOL, May 2026](https://www.ciol.com/startups/karnataka-scales-elevate-to-back-more-early-stage-startups-11870143)
- Over 10 years ELEVATE has supported more than 1,250 startups with about ₹292.57 crore in committed grants. That includes 330 women-led ventures (₹77.39 crore) and 464 startups outside Bengaluru (about ₹102.89 crore). Minister Priyank Kharge's own quote says "over 1,200 startups", which doesn't match exactly — [CIOL, May 2026](https://www.ciol.com/startups/karnataka-scales-elevate-to-back-more-early-stage-startups-11870143)
- Besides cash, winners get mentorship, incubation, access to Centres of Excellence and TBIs, and links to reimbursements (patents, GST, quality certification, marketing). CIOL ties these to Karnataka Startup Policy 2022–27 — [CIOL, May 2026](https://www.ciol.com/startups/karnataka-scales-elevate-to-back-more-early-stage-startups-11870143)
- 2026 eligibility (search-result text): the startup must be registered in Karnataka, have a founding team of at least two people, and be within 10 years of incorporation. Startups that won ELEVATE in the past three years can't apply. A startup may flag several tracks, but its total grant can't exceed ₹50 lakh — [YourStory, Apr 2026](https://yourstory.com/2026/04/karnataka-elevate-25th-call-startup-grant-2026); [StartupGrantsIndia (secondary)](https://www.startupgrantsindia.com/elevate-2026-startup-karnataka-grant-in-aid)
- The 2026 deadline is reported three ways: 21 June 2026 in a Karnataka DIPR post, 25 June 2026 5 PM in a listing, and 15 July 2026 in another listing — [DIPR Karnataka on X](https://x.com/KarnatakaVarthe/status/2057793112202793320); [StartupGrantsIndia (secondary)](https://www.startupgrantsindia.com/elevate-2026-startup-karnataka-grant-in-aid)
- ELEVATE 2025 (previous call): applications ran about 15 Aug – 15 Sep 2025 (secondary). Eligibility was a Karnataka-registered Pvt Ltd, partnership or LLP, ≤10 years old, with turnover never above ₹100 crore in any FY — [StartupGrantsIndia (secondary)](https://www.startupgrantsindia.com/elevate-2025-challenge-grant-in-aid-by-startup-karnataka-110); [BenefitStack (secondary)](https://benefitstack.in/blog/karnataka-elevate-idea2poc-grant)
- ELEVATE 2025 results: Karnataka funded 146 startups, including 103 ELEVATE 2025 winners, with total committed grants of ₹38.85 crore (from search-result text; the article blocked fetch) — [Deccan Herald](https://www.deccanherald.com/india/karnataka/karnataka-government-to-fund-146-startups-under-elevate-programme-3865852)

**Karnataka Startup Policy 2025–2030**
- The cabinet approved the policy with a ₹518.27 crore outlay. It targets 25,000 new startups in five years, 10,000 of them outside Bengaluru. The state already had over 18,000 startups — [CIOL, 10 Nov 2025](https://www.ciol.com/news/karnataka-startup-policy-20252030-targets-25000-startups-beyond-bengaluru-10643057); approval date reported as 6 Nov 2025 by [Entrepreneur India](https://www.entrepreneur.com/en-in/news-and-trends/karnataka-cabinet-approves-startup-policy-2025-2030-with/499357)
- Reported incentives:
  - one-time capital grants up to ₹50 lakh for private incubation centres
  - PF/ESI reimbursement of ₹3,000 per employee per month for the first two years, capped at ₹12 lakh per firm
  - subsidised cloud storage and R&D
  - state-subsidised certifications, training and mentorship-linked assistance
  - government acting as "first customer" where procurement is viable
  - incentives tied to outcomes such as productisation and industry partnerships

  Source: [CIOL, Nov 2025](https://www.ciol.com/news/karnataka-startup-policy-20252030-targets-25000-startups-beyond-bengaluru-10643057)
- Sector focus is deeptech (AI, blockchain, quantum, semiconductor design). Priority nodes outside Bengaluru are Mysuru, Mangaluru, Hubballi-Dharwad and Kalaburagi — [CIOL, Nov 2025](https://www.ciol.com/news/karnataka-startup-policy-20252030-targets-25000-startups-beyond-bengaluru-10643057); [AIM](https://analyticsindiamag.com/ai-news-updates/karnataka-cabinet-approves-startup-policy-to-establish-25000-startups-over-five-years/)

**Startup India Seed Fund Scheme (SISFS)**
- SISFS offers up to ₹20 lakh as a grant for proof of concept/prototype, plus up to ₹50 lakh in debt/convertible instruments for scale-up. It is delivered through incubators in milestone-based tranches — [SISFS portal](https://seedfund.startupindia.gov.in/); [SRF Capital (secondary)](https://srfcapital.studio/resources/articles/startup-india-seed-fund-what-happened)
- The official portal notice gave a final extended deadline of 31 May 2026 for startup applications, with incubators to finish 100% of selection by 30 June 2026 — [SISFS portal](https://seedfund.startupindia.gov.in/)
- A Sept 2026 review says the scheme is no longer taking applications, disbursements to already-selected startups continue, and no next cycle has been announced — [SRF Capital (secondary)](https://srfcapital.studio/resources/articles/startup-india-seed-fund-what-happened). This is contradicted by a May 2026 current-affairs page claiming an extension to March 2028 with ₹1,000 crore extra — [ClearYourExam (low reliability)](https://clearyourexam.com/current-affairs/government-extends-startup-india-seed-fund-scheme-until-2028)

**DPIIT recognition**
- Eligibility:
  - entity type: Pvt Ltd, partnership, LLP or cooperative society
  - turnover below ₹200 crore in every previous FY (₹300 crore for DeepTech)
  - age: up to 10 years from incorporation (20 for DeepTech)
  - working on innovation or improvement of products, services or processes
  - not formed by splitting or reconstructing an existing business
  - no fee for the recognition certificate

  Source: [Startup India – Recognition page](https://www.startupindia.gov.in/content/sih/en/startupgov/startup_recognition_page.html)
- Section 80-IAC gives a 100% deduction of profits for 3 consecutive years out of the first 10. It needs DPIIT recognition plus an Inter-Ministerial Board certificate, a Pvt Ltd or LLP, and turnover ≤ ₹100 crore. Budget 2025-26 extended the incorporation cutoff to before 1 April 2030 — [Startup India](https://www.startupindia.gov.in/content/sih/en/startupgov/startup_recognition_page.html); [TaxUpdate.in (secondary)](https://taxupdate.in/income-tax/779/section-80-iac-startup-tax-holiday-fy-2025-26-dpiit-100-percent-deduction-section-140-income-tax-act-2025/)
- "Angel tax" (section 56(2)(viib)) is reported as fully abolished from 1 April 2025 — [VirtualAuditor (secondary)](https://virtualauditor.in/learn/income-tax-startups-2026-80iac-esop/)
- One source says the Income-tax Act 2025 (effective 1 April 2026) moves the startup deduction to Section 140. This is single-source — [TaxUpdate.in](https://taxupdate.in/income-tax/779/section-80-iac-startup-tax-holiday-fy-2025-26-dpiit-100-percent-deduction-section-140-income-tax-act-2025/)

**Incubators / programmes (Bengaluru)**
- NSRCEL (IIM Bangalore):
  - The homepage lists Launchpad, Campus Founders and Healthcare Incubation (idea/prototype stage).
  - It also lists the Women Startup Program, Velocity–Kotak BizLabs, Circular Bharat Accelerator and Impact Orbit (growth stage).
  - At fetch time the open calls were the Women Startup Program, Circular Bharat and two Impact Orbit tracks.

  Source: [nsrcel.org](https://www.nsrcel.org/)
- NSRCEL Launchpad is a five-month pre-incubation programme for idea/prototype/MVP founders from anywhere in India. Launchpad 30 was listed as "not accepting applications", and ₹5 lakh grants go to the top 3 incubatees — [IncorpX (secondary)](https://www.incorpx.io/grants/nsrcel-launchpad-30-at-iim-bangalore)
- IISc SID: incubation is open to non-IISc founders, seed capital follows SID norms, and the focus is deep-science startups (detailed info is from about 2020) — [SID IISc](https://sid.iisc.ac.in/?p=1379684); [YourStory 2020](https://yourstory.com/2020/12/iisc-sid-incubator-science-startups)
- NASSCOM 10,000 Startups: a 2026 third-party guide says it takes no equity, is an approved AWS Activate provider, and bundles partner credits (it names Google Cloud up to $100K, HubSpot and DigitalOcean). There is no official confirmation — [GrowthGurukul via echai.ventures (secondary)](https://echai.ventures/startingup/from/growthgurukul)

**Accelerators and cloud credits**
- Google for Startups Accelerator India 2026:
  - three-month, equity-free programme for Seed-to-Series A, AI-first startups headquartered in India
  - themes: Agentic, Multimodal, Physical and Sovereign AI
  - applications opened about 31 Mar 2026
  - 20 startups selected from about 2,500 applications, in the programme's 10th year

  Sources: [IANS, 31 Mar 2026](https://ianslive.in/google-launches-2026-india-accelerator-for-ai-startups--20260331124018); [YourStory](https://yourstory.com/ai-story/google-names-20-ai-first-startups-for-2026-india-accelerator); [Google](https://startup.google.com/accelerator/india/)
- Microsoft for Startups changed on 2 July 2025. Startups without an affiliated investor can get up to $5,000 in Azure credits if new to Azure. Larger credits need an Investor Network referral code — [Microsoft Learn](https://learn.microsoft.com/microsoft-for-startups/changes-microsoft-for-startups). TechRadar reports that Microsoft pulled the self-serve programme offering up to $150,000 (memo dated 27 June 2025) — [TechRadar](https://www.techradar.com/pro/microsoft-pulls-program-that-gave-generous-azure-boost-to-ai-startups)
- AWS Activate Founders tier is $1,000 base. The Portfolio tier, which needs an Org ID from an accelerator, VC or incubator, is reported as up to $100K in older guides and up to $200K in newer ones (secondary, conflicting) — [Northflank](https://northflank.com/blog/how-to-get-free-aws-credits-for-your-startup); [CloudKompas](https://cloudkompas.com/blog/aws-activate-complete-guide-2026)
- Antler India's AI Residency adds a one-time ₹2 lakh grant for expenses plus more than $1M in AI-relevant perks, on top of its equity cheque — [Antler India](https://www.antler.co/location/india)

### Inferences
- **Timing.** ELEVATE calls have run in Aug–Sep 2025 and May–Jul 2026. The next ELEVATE window is therefore plausibly mid-2027, which falls inside or just after DineView's 6-month launch. DineView should prepare a PoC/pilot evidence pack now: live restaurant pilots, diner engagement, dish-capture cost per item. It also needs a ≥2-person founding team registered in Karnataka.
- **Fit.** DineView (AR/3D dish capture plus ordering) fits the ELEVATE General track. It doesn't fit the policy's priority deeptech list (AI/quantum/semiconductor) unless the 3D capture pipeline is positioned as an AI/computer-vision innovation.
- **SISFS.** SISFS is effectively unavailable unless a new cycle is announced. Watch DPIIT notices, not blogs.
- **DPIIT recognition.** This is the cheapest high-value step. It is free and needed for 80-IAC, startup-scheme eligibility and most incubator programmes.
- **Credits.** The biggest cloud and AI credits (AWS Portfolio, Microsoft Investor Network, Google Cloud) are now gated behind an incubator, accelerator or VC referral. Joining NSRCEL, NASSCOM 10K or an angel network early has indirect value as a credit unlock.
- **Policy confusion.** In May 2026 CIOL still tied ELEVATE reimbursements to the 2022–27 policy, even though the 2025–30 policy was approved in Nov 2025. Operational guidelines for 2025–30 incentives may not have been notified yet.

### Gaps
- I couldn't reach the official ELEVATE portal (startup.karnataka.gov.in failed DNS) to confirm the exact 2026 deadline, 2026 turnover cap, tranche structure or 2026 winner count. One aggregator claims "101 selected from 960 applicants" in 2024; this is unverified, so it isn't reported as fact.
- I couldn't fetch SISFS eligibility details from the official site (≤2 years since incorporation, limit on prior government grants, Indian shareholding). Check the scheme guidelines, especially whether an ELEVATE grant would rule a startup out of SISFS if SISFS reopens.
- No current official data for KTech / Karnataka Innovation & Technology Society programmes, NASSCOM 10K 2026 cohort terms, or the official AWS Activate credit caps.
- No official text found for the Karnataka Startup Policy 2025–30 operational guidelines (patent, GST, marketing reimbursements under the new policy).

## 2. Equity funding landscape: Indian restaurant/hospitality tech and vertical SMB SaaS (2024–2026), round sizes, valuations, investors

### Takeaway
Indian restaurant-tech funding is thin and dominated by a few incumbents. Petpooja's ₹137 crore (about $15.5M) round in Sept 2025 is the only sizeable 2025 restaurant-SaaS round found. Most other notable rounds are from 2021–2023: UrbanPiper $24M (2022), DotPe $58M (2022), Thrive with strategic investors (2021–23), and peAR's small AR-menu rounds (2021–23). Overall Indian seed funding was $793M across 433 deals in 2025, with a stable median seed cheque of about $1M through H1 2026, while investor attention shifted hard toward AI. The most direct comparable for DineView, peAR (AR/3D menus with in-restaurant ordering), was backed by angel platforms (IPV, Venture Catalysts, LetsVenture, 1Crowd, Chennai Angels), not tier-1 VCs.

### Cited Findings

**Macro funding (Inc42)**
- Full-year 2025: about $11 Bn across 936+ deals, down about 8% YoY. Seed/early-stage was $793 Mn across 433 deals (funding down 12%). Overall median ticket was $3 Mn (up 11%) — [Inc42, 28 Dec 2025](https://inc42.com/buzz/indian-startup-funding-decline-8-yoy-to-11-bn-in-2025/)
- Bengaluru got $4.5 Bn across 300 deals in 2025 (up 33% from $3.4 Bn), about 41% of national funding. Enterprise tech got $1.8 Bn across 82 deals (deal count roughly halved). Peak XV, Accel and Kalaari were noted as active in early and growth stages — [Inc42, Dec 2025](https://inc42.com/buzz/indian-startup-funding-decline-8-yoy-to-11-bn-in-2025/)
- The seed-stage median ticket held flat at $1 Mn in H1 2025 and Q3 2025 — [Inc42 H1 2025](https://inc42.com/buzz/indian-startup-funding-jumps-8-yoy-to-5-7-bn-in-h1-2025/); [Inc42 Q3 2025](https://inc42.com/buzz/startup-funding-dips-38-to-2-1-bn-in-q3-2025/)
- H1 2026:
  - $5.2 Bn total (down 9% YoY) across 501 deals (up 7%)
  - seed $478 Mn (up 18%) with a median of $1 Mn
  - AI $676 Mn across 57 deals (funding up 317%)
  - Bengaluru $2.7 Bn across 165 deals, more than half of national funding
  - IvyCap quote: investors are shifting toward companies whose edge is technology rather than distribution

  Source: [Inc42, 30 Jun 2026](https://inc42.com/features/indian-startup-funding-slips-9-to-5-2-bn-in-h1-2026/)

### Inferences
- Antler's terms imply a post-money valuation of about $3.9M (about ₹36 crore) for an Antler-stage company. That is a useful upper anchor for a pre-revenue Indian pre-seed in 2025–26. Angel-network rounds such as peAR's ₹2.5–4.2 crore rounds were smaller.
- A realistic DineView path is: angel round or angel platform (IPV, Venture Catalysts, LetsVenture, 1Crowd, Chennai Angels have all backed a restaurant-AR company), or a pre-seed fund (Antler India, Peak XV Surge for AI-framed pitches). Then a roughly $1M seed (the Indian median) once traction exists.
- Strategic investors matter in this vertical. Swiggy and Zomato invested in UrbanPiper, and Jubilant and Coca-Cola invested in Thrive. F&B chains or beverage and FMCG brands (menu-placement and upsell data) are plausible strategic angels or partners for DineView.
- Thrive's losses and its exit from food delivery, plus peAR's lack of follow-on rounds after 2023, are cautionary data points. Investors are likely to probe DineView on unit economics of 3D capture and on retention, not just novelty.
- The 2026 investor tilt to AI (AI funding up 317% in H1 2026) suggests DineView should lead its pitch with its AI/computer-vision 3D capture pipeline and data (dish engagement, upsell), not "AR menu".

## 4. Compliance A: DPDP Act 2023 and DPDP Rules 2025 (diner phone numbers, orders)

### Takeaway
The DPDP Rules were notified on 14 Nov 2025. Most substantive duties (notice, security, breach reporting, retention, grievance) commence 18 months after publication, around 13–14 May 2027, which lands right around DineView's launch window. Consent-manager rules commence around Nov 2026. A Jan 2026 proposal to compress the timeline to 12 months for significant data fiduciaries has not been notified as of Oct 2026. Penalties reach ₹250 crore for security failures. DineView should build DPDP-grade consent, notice, logging and breach processes from day one.

### Cited Findings
- The DPDP Act was enacted on 11 Aug 2023, and the DPDP Rules 2025 were notified on 14 Nov 2025 after 6,915 consultation inputs — [PIB backgrounder, 17 Nov 2025](https://static.pib.gov.in/WriteReadData/specificdocs/documents/2025/nov/doc20251117695301.pdf). Some secondary sources say 13 Nov 2025 — [Seqrite](https://www.seqrite.com/blog/dpdp-rules-are-here-what-changed-from-the-draft/)
- Commencement (Rule 1):
  - Rules 1, 2 and 17–21 (definitions, Data Protection Board) are in force on publication.
  - Rule 4 (Consent Managers) comes into force one year after publication.
  - Rules 3, 5–16, 22 and 23 come into force eighteen months after publication.

  Source: [DPDP Rules 2025, Gazette text via MeitY](https://www.meity.gov.in/static/uploads/2025/11/53450e6e5dc0bfa85ebd78686cadad39.pdf)
- In Jan 2026 MeitY was reported to be considering a cut from 18 to 12 months for certain provisions for significant data fiduciaries — [Business Standard, Jan 2026](https://www.business-standard.com/technology/tech-news/meity-may-cut-compliance-timeline-for-key-dpdp-rules-to-12-months-126012201293_1.html). As of an Oct 2026 review, no notification making that change had been found, so 13 May 2027 remains the operative date — [dpdprules.org](https://dpdprules.org/blog/dpdp-18-months-to-12-months-proposal); [Mondaq](https://www.mondaq.com/india/data-protection/1773554/meity-plans-to-cut-short-dpdp-compliance-timeline-and-notify-cross-border-restrictions-for-sdfs)
- Key definitions:
  - A Data Fiduciary decides why and how personal data is processed.
  - A Data Processor processes on a Fiduciary's behalf.
  - Consent Managers must be Indian companies.

  Source: [PIB backgrounder](https://static.pib.gov.in/WriteReadData/specificdocs/documents/2025/nov/doc20251117695301.pdf)
- Rule 3 (notice): the notice must be understandable on its own. It must give an itemised description of the personal data and the specified purpose(s), with a description of the goods or services. It must also give a link and means to withdraw consent "with the ease of doing so being comparable to that with which such consent was given", to exercise rights, and to complain to the Board — [DPDP Rules](https://www.meity.gov.in/static/uploads/2025/11/53450e6e5dc0bfa85ebd78686cadad39.pdf)
- Rule 6 (security) sets these minimums:
  - encryption, obfuscation, masking or virtual tokens
  - access control
  - logs, monitoring and review to detect unauthorised access
  - backups for continuity
  - retain logs and personal data for one year for detection and investigation
  - contract clauses with Data Processors requiring safeguards

  Source: [DPDP Rules](https://www.meity.gov.in/static/uploads/2025/11/53450e6e5dc0bfa85ebd78686cadad39.pdf)
- Rule 7 (breach): notify each affected Data Principal "without delay" in plain language. Notify the Board without delay, followed by a detailed report "within seventy-two hours of becoming aware of the breach" (extendable by the Board on request) — [DPDP Rules](https://www.meity.gov.in/static/uploads/2025/11/53450e6e5dc0bfa85ebd78686cadad39.pdf)
- Rule 8(3): retain personal data, traffic data and processing logs for at least one year from processing, then erase unless another law requires longer. The official illustration says an e-book platform must keep order, payment and delivery data and logs for at least one year even if the user deletes the account — [DPDP Rules](https://www.meity.gov.in/static/uploads/2025/11/53450e6e5dc0bfa85ebd78686cadad39.pdf)
- Third Schedule: the 3-year inactivity erasure (with 48-hour advance warning) applies only to specified classes. These are e-commerce entities with ≥2 crore registered users in India, online gaming intermediaries with ≥50 lakh users, and social media intermediaries — [DPDP Rules](https://www.meity.gov.in/static/uploads/2025/11/53450e6e5dc0bfa85ebd78686cadad39.pdf)
- Rule 9 and grievances: publish business contact details of a designated person or DPO on the website or app. Respond to grievances within a reasonable period "not exceeding ninety days". Access, correction and erasure requests must also be handled within 90 days — [DPDP Rules](https://www.meity.gov.in/static/uploads/2025/11/53450e6e5dc0bfa85ebd78686cadad39.pdf); [PIB backgrounder](https://static.pib.gov.in/WriteReadData/specificdocs/documents/2025/nov/doc20251117695301.pdf)
- Children's data needs verifiable parental or guardian consent, except for specified essential services — [PIB backgrounder](https://static.pib.gov.in/WriteReadData/specificdocs/documents/2025/nov/doc20251117695301.pdf)
- Penalties: up to ₹250 crore for failing to maintain reasonable security safeguards; up to ₹200 crore each for failing to notify a breach or breaching children's-data obligations; up to ₹50 crore for other violations — [PIB backgrounder](https://static.pib.gov.in/WriteReadData/specificdocs/documents/2025/nov/doc20251117695301.pdf)

### Inferences
- **Role split.** For diner data collected on behalf of a restaurant (name, phone, order history at that outlet), the restaurant is likely the Data Fiduciary and DineView a Data Processor. Each restaurant contract therefore needs a data-processing clause (Rule 6(1)(f) requires security clauses in Fiduciary–Processor contracts). Where DineView uses diner data for its own purposes (cross-restaurant profiles, analytics, marketing, product improvement), DineView becomes a Data Fiduciary in its own right and needs its own notice and consent.
- **Product implications:**
  - Make the phone number optional, or collect it only when needed (OTP for order status or bill).
  - Show a standalone itemised notice before collection.
  - Keep marketing consent separate and unticked.
  - Make withdrawal as easy as opt-in (for example a one-tap link in WhatsApp/SMS).
  - Keep order logs for at least 1 year.
  - Encrypt or mask phone numbers in the restaurant dashboard.
  - Have a 72-hour breach runbook.
  - Publish a grievance contact.
- **Children.** Diners may include minors. Avoid knowingly collecting children's data, for example with an age self-declaration for marketing opt-ins.
- **Timing.** May 2027 is about when DineView's 6-month launch ends, so building compliant flows now avoids a retrofit. Enterprise restaurant chains will likely ask for DPDP clauses in vendor contracts before then.

### Gaps
- No official guidance found on how DPDP treats a SaaS vendor serving many restaurants (processor vs joint fiduciary). A lawyer's opinion is needed.
- I didn't check whether any small-entity or startup exemption under section 17(3) of the Act has been notified for startups.
- The cross-border transfer restrictions (Rule 15) and the Significant Data Fiduciary criteria weren't analysed.

## 5. Compliance B: GST (registration, 18% on SaaS, restaurant-ordering traps)

### Takeaway
SaaS subscriptions and the 3D capture service are taxed at 18% (GST 2.0, effective 22 Sep 2025, kept SaaS/IT at 18%). The registration threshold for services is ₹20 lakh turnover, but in practice B2B restaurant customers will want GST invoices. The big trap is section 9(5). If DineView's platform is treated as an e-commerce operator through which restaurant services are supplied, DineView itself could owe 5% GST on the restaurant's food bills, paid in cash with no ITC. Ordering and payment flows should be designed so the restaurant remains the supplier and collector.

### Cited Findings
- GST 2.0 took effect on 22 Sep 2025 and kept SaaS, IT and OIDAR services at 18% (CGST 9% + SGST 9% intra-state, or IGST 18% inter-state). Exports of services are zero-rated — [Kintsugi GST guide (secondary)](https://trykintsugi.com/sales-tax-guides/apac/india); [Xflow (secondary)](https://www.xflowpay.com/blog/gst-on-software-services)
- Registration is required once aggregate turnover exceeds ₹20 lakh for services (₹40 lakh for goods; lower in special-category states) — [Kintsugi (secondary)](https://trykintsugi.com/sales-tax-guides/apac/india)
- Restaurant services were notified under section 9(5) by Notification 17/2021. From 1 Jan 2022, the e-commerce operator (ECO) pays GST at 5% on restaurant services supplied through it, even if the restaurant is unregistered — [CBIC Circular 167/23/2021 (GST Council)](https://www.gstcouncil.gov.in/sites/default/files/2024-06/circular-167-2021-gst.pdf); [ClearTax](https://cleartax.in/s/gst-on-service-supplied-restaurants-ecommerce-operators)
- That 9(5) tax must be paid in cash with no ITC, and ECOs don't collect TCS (section 52) or file GSTR-8 for those restaurant services — [CBIC Circular 167/23/2021](https://www.gstcouncil.gov.in/sites/default/files/2024-06/circular-167-2021-gst.pdf); [ClearTax](https://cleartax.in/s/gst-on-notified-services-ecommerce-operators-95)
- For other supplies through an ECO, TCS under section 52 is 0.5% (0.25% CGST + 0.25% SGST, or 0.5% IGST) — [CBIC Circular 167/23/2021](https://www.gstcouncil.gov.in/sites/default/files/2024-06/circular-167-2021-gst.pdf); [Taxscan](https://www.taxscan.in/top-stories/gstr-8-filing-guide-tcs-compliance-for-e-commerce-operators-common-pitfalls-1445138)
- Restaurant services at "specified premises" (hotels with room tariff ≥ ₹7,500) are carved out of the 9(5) notification — [Professional Utilities (secondary)](https://www.professionalutilities.com/blogs/section-9-5-gst-supply-notified-services.php)
- None of the reviewed sources addresses whether a dine-in QR-ordering software vendor is an ECO for 9(5) purposes — [search review; e.g. Terra Insight](https://www.terra-insight.com/insights/gst-section-9-5-aggregator-restaurant-liability/)

### Inferences
- **Lowest-risk structure.** The restaurant is merchant-of-record and issues the food bill (ideally from its own POS). Diner payments settle directly to the restaurant's own gateway or UPI account. DineView invoices the restaurant separately for SaaS and capture fees at 18%. This keeps DineView a software or technology service provider, not an ECO collecting consideration for restaurant services.
- **Higher-risk structure.** DineView runs a consumer-facing checkout that collects payment into its own account and settles to restaurants. This looks like the Swiggy/Zomato model and could trigger 9(5) (5% on all food GMV) plus the RBI PA issues in section 6.
- GST registration is effectively needed from day one. Restaurants will want ITC on SaaS fees, and inter-state customers would raise further questions (see Gaps).

### Gaps
- No ruling, circular or advance ruling found on whether QR/table-ordering SaaS for dine-in counts as an "electronic commerce operator" under section 2(45) CGST and 9(5). This needs a CA or GST lawyer opinion.
- I didn't verify whether inter-state SaaS supplies below ₹20 lakh still force compulsory registration (exemption notification), or e-invoicing thresholds.

## 6. Compliance C: Payments — does a QR ordering app need an RBI Payment Aggregator licence?

### Takeaway
A non-bank Payment Aggregator (PA) needs RBI authorisation, with ₹15 crore net worth at application. That is not realistic for DineView, and it isn't needed if DineView never handles funds. The Sept 2025 PA Master Direction tightened marketplace and split-settlement rules. Razorpay Route (split payments) now requires the platform to have more than ₹40 lakh domestic turnover and a payer-payee transparency declaration, so a pre-revenue DineView likely can't use Route. The workable path is for each restaurant to be the merchant with its own PA account: onboarded through a PA's partner/sub-merchant APIs (e.g. the Razorpay Partner programme), or by connecting the restaurant's existing gateway or UPI.

### Cited Findings
- RBI issued the Master Direction on Regulation of Payment Aggregators on 15 Sep 2025. It is effective immediately and consolidates and supersedes all PA directions since 17 Mar 2020 — [Cyril Amarchand Mangaldas alert, 23 Sep 2025](https://www.cyrilshroff.com/wp-content/uploads/2025/10/Client-Alert-RBI-Introduces-Consolidated-Framework-for-Payment-Aggregators-3.pdf)
- PA categories:
  - PA-Online (PA-O): the acceptance device and the payment instrument are not in close proximity
  - PA-Physical (PA-P): both are physically present in close proximity
  - PA-Cross-Border (PA-CB)

  PA-P-only entities had to apply by 31 Dec 2025 or wind up by 28 Feb 2026 — [CAM alert](https://www.cyrilshroff.com/wp-content/uploads/2025/10/Client-Alert-RBI-Introduces-Consolidated-Framework-for-Payment-Aggregators-3.pdf)
- Marketplaces are now included in the merchant definition, so PAs can settle with marketplaces. The PA must ensure it processes and settles only for sellers onboarded by those marketplaces — [CAM alert](https://www.cyrilshroff.com/wp-content/uploads/2025/10/Client-Alert-RBI-Introduces-Consolidated-Framework-for-Payment-Aggregators-3.pdf). A marketplace is defined as an e-commerce entity providing an IT platform to facilitate transactions between buyers and sellers, and a PA cannot also be a marketplace — [Mondaq](https://www.mondaq.com/india/financial-services/1727528/rbi-master-direction-on-digital-payment-aggregators-understanding-compliance-requirements-and-industry-implications); [Saraf Partners](https://sarafpartners.com/rbi-issues-the-reserve-bank-of-india-regulation-of-payment-aggregators-directions-2025/)
- Settlement to a third party on a merchant's direction is now allowed only for merchants with annual turnover above ₹40 lakh (or export turnover above ₹5 lakh). The third party must be the payee that interfaces with the payer for the underlying transaction. Delivery-vs-payment transactions are no longer exempt; only cash on delivery is — [CAM alert](https://www.cyrilshroff.com/wp-content/uploads/2025/10/Client-Alert-RBI-Introduces-Consolidated-Framework-for-Payment-Aggregators-3.pdf)
- Merchants with turnover ≤ ₹40 lakh (or exports ≤ ₹5 lakh) can be onboarded with alternate CDD (PAN/Form 60, contact-point verification, certified OVDs). PAs must allocate MCCs, verify merchant bank accounts and monitor continuously — [CAM alert](https://www.cyrilshroff.com/wp-content/uploads/2025/10/Client-Alert-RBI-Introduces-Consolidated-Framework-for-Payment-Aggregators-3.pdf)
- Merchants onboarded up to 31 Dec 2025 must be KYC-compliant by 14 Sep 2026, and all new merchants from 1 Jan 2026 — [CAM alert](https://www.cyrilshroff.com/wp-content/uploads/2025/10/Client-Alert-RBI-Introduces-Consolidated-Framework-for-Payment-Aggregators-3.pdf)
- A non-bank PA needs a minimum net worth of ₹15 crore at application — [MediaNama, Sep 2025](https://www.medianama.com/2025/09/223-explained-rbi-master-direction-payment-aggregators/)
- Razorpay Route criteria under the Sept 2025 PA guidelines — [Razorpay Route docs](https://razorpay.com/docs/payments/route/):
  - the business must have domestic turnover above ₹40 lakh (via GSTR-3B) or export turnover above ₹5 lakh (via FIRC) in FY26 or FY25
  - each linked account must deal directly with the customer (payer-payee transparency), shown by a written use-case confirmation that Razorpay approves
  - accounts that hadn't submitted proofs by 31 Dec 2025 had Route disabled
- Razorpay's Partner programme lets software platforms onboard customers as sub-merchants through APIs, with KYC inside the partner's platform, and partners earn a share of transaction revenue — [Razorpay sub-merchant onboarding docs](https://razorpay.com/docs/partners/aggregators/onboarding-api/); [Razorpay partners](https://razorpay.com/us/partners/)
- Xpress onboarding lets a sub-merchant accept payments through Payment Links and QR before full KYC, capped at ₹50,000 GMV (₹5 lakh once GST is verified) — [Razorpay Xpress onboarding](https://razorpay.com/docs/partners/aggregators/xpress-onboarding)
- A "Service Partner"/reseller variant has restaurants create their own Razorpay account via referral, with the partner earning commission — [Razorpay reseller docs](https://razorpay.com/docs/partners/resellers/add-submerchants)

### Inferences
- DineView doesn't need a PA licence if it is purely a technology layer: the restaurant is the merchant, the PA settles directly to the restaurant's bank account, and DineView never receives or holds diner funds. This also lines up with the GST low-risk structure in section 5.
- Collecting diner payments into DineView's own account and paying restaurants out would look like payment aggregation (unauthorised) or a marketplace. It would also hit the ₹40 lakh turnover gate for Route-style splits. DineView should avoid this, at least until it has scale and legal advice.
- Monetisation options without touching funds: subscription invoices (18% GST), the PA partner revenue share (Razorpay Partner), or a per-order platform fee invoiced to the restaurant monthly.
- Many target restaurants already use POS-integrated payments (Petpooja and others). An integration that pushes the order to the restaurant's POS and lets the restaurant collect payment may be both simpler and more compliant.

### Gaps
- I didn't read the RBI Master Direction text itself (only the CAM, Mondaq and Saraf summaries). No explicit RBI statement was found on technology service providers that never touch funds.
- No source confirmed how Cashfree (Easy Split/marketplace) or PayU applied the ₹40 lakh gate after Sept 2025.
- Whether a diner scanning a table QR and paying on their phone browser counts as PA-O or PA-P wasn't confirmed.

## 7. Compliance D: FSSAI — menu platform vs restaurant responsibilities

### Takeaway
The restaurant is the Food Business Operator (FBO) and is responsible for the accuracy of food information. Under the FSS (Labelling & Display) Regulations 2020, restaurants with a central licence or 10+ outlets must show calories (kcal per serving and serving size) on menus, along with allergen and other information. E-commerce FBOs (delivery/marketplace platforms) need a central FSSAI licence. Since 1 July 2022 they must enable display of calories, nutrition, allergens and ingredients that restaurants feed in. A dine-in menu SaaS that doesn't sell food on its own account is probably not an e-commerce FBO, but DineView's nutrition and allergen display is effectively a compliance tool for chains, and the data must come from, and be signed off by, the restaurant.

### Cited Findings
- Regulation 9 of the FSS (Labelling & Display) Regulations 2020 covers food service establishments with a central licence or outlets at 10 or more locations. They must display calorific value (kcal per serving and serving size) against menu items, and display allergen information. Event caterers and premises operating under 60 days a year are exempt — [S.S. Rana & Co.](https://ssrana.in/articles/display-calorie-values-menus-fssai/); [FSSAI Guidance Note on Labelling, Feb 2022](https://fssai.gov.in/upload/uploadfiles/files/Guidance_Note_Labelling_23_02_2022.pdf)
- An FSSAI clarification reported in April 2024 says menu cards and boards must identify allergens in each dish and show calories and veg/non-veg logos. Nutritional and organic information can be given in booklets, handouts or on the website (search-result text; the source blocked fetch) — [Legality Simplified](https://www.legalitysimplified.com/fssai-clarifies-on-display-of-information-in-restaurants/)
- On 14 June 2022 FSSAI directed e-commerce FBOs to "enable provisions in their online platforms including mobile applications" for displaying calories, nutrition, allergens and ingredients for restaurants covered by Reg 9. Restaurants "feed and update such information in respect of each dish". Effective 1 July 2022 — [SCC Online, 16 Jun 2022](https://www.scconline.com/blog/post/2022/06/16/fssai-directs-e-commerce-food-business-operators-for-display-of-nutritional-information-on-their-website/)
- FSSAI's e-commerce FBO guidelines (Feb 2017) cover licensing, the platform/restaurant liability split and display duties:
  - They define inventory-based and marketplace-based e-commerce FBOs. E-commerce FBOs need a Central Licensing Authority licence.
  - Pure listing/directory services may not need a licence unless they fall in the marketplace model. Platforms facilitating orders or transactions do need one.
  - Platforms must state that liability rests with the restaurant or brand owner.
  - Sellers must display their FSSAI licence/registration and hygiene rating.

  Sources: [USDA GAIN summary, Feb 2017](https://gain.fas.usda.gov/Recent%20GAIN%20Publications/FSSAI%20Publishes%20Guidelines%20for%20Operations%20of%20E-Commerce%20FBOs_New%20Delhi_India_2-24-2017.pdf); [YourStory, Feb 2017](https://yourstory.com/2017/02/fssai-e-commerce)
- One law-firm summary says the 2022 platform order applies to FBOs with more than ₹20 crore annual gross revenue and more than ten licensed outlets. This is unverified against FSSAI text — [Ahlawat & Associates](https://www.ahlawatassociates.com/news/display-of-information-by-food-business-operators)

### Inferences
- **Status.** DineView running a single restaurant's own dine-in menu (restaurant is seller and server, no delivery, no DineView marketplace) is most likely a software vendor, not an e-commerce FBO. It would need no FSSAI licence of its own. This changes if DineView launches a consumer-facing multi-restaurant discovery and ordering app (the marketplace model), which would bring a central licence and display duties.
- **Contracts and UI.** Restaurants should be contractually responsible for menu, nutrition, allergen and veg/non-veg data. DineView's UI should show the restaurant's FSSAI licence number and a disclaimer that food information is provided by the restaurant. Calories, serving size, allergens and the veg/non-veg logo should be mandatory fields for chain (10+ outlet) customers. This is a selling point: it helps chains meet Reg 9 on a digital menu.
- **3D models.** These should be representative of actual serving size and appearance to avoid misleading-claims risk (inferred from the 2017 guideline's ban on misleading information).

### Gaps
- I didn't fetch the current consolidated text of the FSS (Labelling & Display) Regulations 2020 or the 2024 clarification directly. The 2017 e-commerce guidelines may have been superseded or amended by later FSSAI orders; this wasn't checked.
- No source addresses whether a digital QR menu legally substitutes for the physical menu card or board display required by Reg 9.
- Not researched (outside the brief, but relevant): Consumer Protection (E-Commerce) Rules 2020 obligations if DineView becomes a marketplace, Karnataka Shops & Establishments registration, Professional Tax, and the IT Act/CERT-In 6-hour incident-reporting direction.
