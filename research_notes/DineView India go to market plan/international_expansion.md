# DineView international expansion: which countries after India, and why (as of Oct 2026)

Scope note: research done 27 Oct 2026. Many sources are vendor blogs or news reports, not regulator texts, and they are labelled as such. Older data is marked with its year. Where a number could not be found it is listed under Gaps, not estimated.

## 1. Calorie and allergen menu-labelling laws by market, and whether digital (QR) menus count

### Takeaway
Saudi Arabia is the strongest regulatory wedge. Since 1 July 2025, SFDA rules require restaurants and cafes nationwide to show calories, 14 allergens, a high-sodium icon, caffeine content and a "calorie burn" activity equivalent. The rules cover print, digital and delivery-app menus, and no size threshold was found. India (FSSAI, chains with 10+ outlets or a central licence), the UK (250+ employees), the US (20+ locations) and Australian states (20+ outlets in a state or 50+ nationally) mandate calories only for large chains. Dubai's 2019 calorie mandate was deferred and made voluntary, and no reinstatement was found. Singapore's rule covers only freshly prepared drinks (Nutri-Grade). No regulator found says outright that a QR-only menu is enough. Saudi Arabia, the UK and Singapore do explicitly bring digital, online or app menus into scope.

### Cited Findings

**Saudi Arabia (SFDA)**
- The first mandate took effect around 31 Dec 2018 (The National, published 1 Jan 2019). Restaurants, cafes, ice-cream parlours, juice vendors, bakeries, sweet shops and cafeterias had to show calories at cashier desks, on menu boards and table menus, at drive-thrus, and in "phone and web applications". A grace period applied and SFDA published a guidebook. No penalty amounts were given. — [The National, 1 Jan 2019](https://www.thenationalnews.com/uae/health/saudi-arabia-brings-in-mandatory-calorie-labels-on-menus-1.808556)
- Updated technical rules took effect 1 July 2025. They require detailed nutrition information on menus, a salt-shaker icon on high-sodium meals, caffeine content for beverages, and an estimate of the time needed to burn off each item's calories. They cover physical and digital menus, including food delivery apps. The full technical guide sits on SFDA's Mwasfah portal, and SFDA told businesses to comply "to avoid penalties". Penalties are not specified. — [Gulf News, 2 Jul 2025](https://gulfnews.com/amp/story/world%2Fgulf%2Fsaudi%2Fsaudi-arabia-enforces-new-menu-rules-to-flag-salt-caffeine-and-calorie-burn-1.500183654)
- A vendor summary (Foodics, a Saudi POS company, 30 Jun 2025) says the rules apply to "all food and beverage establishments across the Kingdom": restaurants, cafes, bakeries, food trucks, delivery platforms and caterers. Required items are calories per item, fat, sugar, sodium, caffeine, the 14 main allergens, a high-sodium icon and a physical-activity equivalent. Formats covered are print menus, menu boards, digital menus and apps, and delivery websites and apps. No size threshold is given and QR codes are not mentioned. — [Foodics](https://www.foodics.com/calorie-counts-on-menus-new-sfda-regulations-for-restaurants/)
- The dates conflict. A label-software vendor lists the allergen-disclosure rule (SFDA.FD 2605) as effective 1 Jan 2025, and the caffeine, physical-activity and high-sodium rules as effective 1 Jul 2025. — [Food Label Maker (vendor)](https://foodlabelmaker.com/blog/labeling-regulations/menu-calorie-labeling-saudi-arabia/). Saudi allergen-labelling legislation itself predates 2025: there is an academic survey of allergic consumers' responses to the "new allergen-labelling legislation in Saudi Arabia". — [FAO AGRIS record](https://agris.fao.org/search/fr/records/65de5ec80f3e94b9e5ce7ccf)
- Compliance gap (pre-2025 data): a 2023 study of 120 "healthy food" restaurants found that 43.3% of healthy foods on delivery apps did not comply with SFDA calorie-labelling policies. — [Frontiers in Nutrition, 2023](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/nutrition/articles/10.3389/fnut.2023.1281293/pdf)
- Vendors are already selling SFDA-compliance QR menus. One example is an Odoo QR menu module with indicators for the 14 SFDA allergens. — [Odoo Apps listing](https://apps.odoo.com/apps/modules/18.0/an_menu); also [Food Label Maker SFDA offer](https://foodlabelmaker.com/saudi-sfda-offer/)

**UAE: Dubai (Dubai Municipality)**
- In 2019 Dubai Municipality announced mandatory calorie counts on menus. Restaurants, cafeterias and cafes with more than 5 branches were to comply by November 2019, and all other restaurants, caterers and hotels by January 2020. — [Gulf News, 2019](https://gulfnews.com/uae/government/dubai-eateries-should-have-calorie-count-on-menus-1.1558165579667)
- The Dubai government then deferred the mandatory rule by two years, making it voluntary. — [Gulf News](https://gulfnews.com/uae/calorie-display-in-dubai-menus-delayed-for-two-years-1.65352261). No source found showing it has been reinstated as of Oct 2026 (searches for 2025 and 2026 status returned only 2016–2019 articles).
- Since 2016 Dubai Municipality has run a "healthy dish" logo that restaurants can put on menus. — [The National](https://thenationalnews.com/uae/health/logo-will-help-diners-make-healthier-choices-1.44721)

**UAE: Abu Dhabi**
- No ADAFSA rule requiring calorie labelling was found. ADAFSA's documented role is food-safety ratings: "Zadna Rating" stickers on 6,900 establishments (June 2023), which also report participation in ADPHC's Sehhi programme. — [WAM](https://www.wam.ae/en/article/hszrha87-adafsa-install-zadna-rating-labels-6900-food)
- The Abu Dhabi Public Health Centre (ADPHC) runs the voluntary SEHHI healthy-menu programme. It trained staff at 600 establishments registered with DCT Abu Dhabi to show calories and macronutrients (article undated). — [Gulf News](https://gulfnews.com/uae/abu-dhabi-trains-hotel-restaurant-workers-about-nutritional-information-on-menus-1.87736107)
- A July 2026 Abu Dhabi study (5,002 UAE residents) found diners ordered an average of 114 fewer calories per meal when menus showed calories and nutrition information. — [Khaleej Times, Jul 2026](https://www.khaleejtimes.com/uae/new-abu-dhabi-study-calorie-labels-help-cut-meal-orders-114-calories)

**Qatar**
- A joint Ministry of Commerce/MoPH circular, based on Consumer Protection Law No. 8 of 2008, tells restaurants and cafes to describe food clearly on menus. It requires calorie counts only from franchise chains. The circular's date was not found in the results. — [Qatar Tribune](https://www.qatar-tribune.com/article/128298/NATION/MEC-and-MoPH-launch-initiative-to-regulate-restaurants-cafes-39-menus)
- In 2015 a broader law requiring ingredients and calories was "under study". — [The Peninsula, Feb 2015](https://thepeninsulaqatar.com/article/11/02/2015/new-law-on-eateries-under-study). No evidence was found that it was enacted.

**Singapore (MOH/HPB/SFA)**
- Since 30 Dec 2023, Nutri-Grade requirements cover freshly prepared beverages (freshly brewed coffee, juices, bubble tea) at F&B outlets, caterers and digital platforms. Grades C and D must be labelled, grades A and B are optional, sugar content of toppings must be shown on menus, a simplified mark can be used on multi-drink menus, and advertising of D-grade drinks is banned. — [MOH](https://www.moh.gov.sg/newsroom/extension-of-nutri-grade-requirements-to-freshly-prepared-beverages-from-30-december-2023/); [Asia News Network](https://asianews.network/spore-drink-outlets-must-label-beverages-high-in-sugar-saturated-fat-on-their-menus/)
- No Singapore rule requiring calorie or allergen labelling on restaurant food menus was found.

**UK**
- The Calorie Labelling (Out of Home Sector) (England) Regulations 2021 have been in force since 6 April 2022 for businesses with 250+ employees in England. Menus must show kcal and portion size, plus a "2,000 kcal a day" statement. — [Citation](https://www.citation.co.uk/calorie-labelling-law-to-come-into-force-from-6-april/)
- Official guidance counts part-time staff in the headcount and generally counts franchisees with the franchisor. Electronic menus, online menus and third-party delivery-app menus all count as "menus", and calories must sit next to each item's description or price at every point of choice. Any website or app selling the food (a "remote provider") must show calories "irrespective of the size of their business", and the food business must supply the data. Local authorities enforce through improvement notices; failing to comply with a notice is an offence, with a £2,500 fixed monetary penalty as an alternative to prosecution, and ±20% tolerance on values. QR codes are not mentioned. — [GOV.UK implementation guidance](https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/calorie-labelling-in-the-out-of-home-sector/calorie-labelling-in-the-out-of-home-sector-implementation-guidance)
- Allergens: on 5 March 2025 the FSA issued best-practice guidance (England, Wales, Northern Ireland) recommending that restaurants and cafes give written information on the 14 allergens alongside a conversation with the customer. The guidance does not change the law, and the FSA offers free allergen icons and matrix templates. — [FSA](https://www.food.gov.uk/business-guidance/allergen-information-for-non-prepacked-foods-best-practice-introduction); [FSA news](https://www.food.gov.uk/news-alerts/news/updated-industry-guidance-issued-for-food-allergen-information-in-the-out-of-home-sector)
- 94% of food businesses say they give written allergen information, but only 51% of adults with food hypersensitivities received it on their last purchase of non-prepacked food (2025 FSA research). — [FSA science report](https://science.food.gov.uk/article/142304.pdf)
- Under current UK law restaurants may tell customers about the 14 allergens verbally or in writing. "Owen's Law", which would make written allergen information at the point of ordering mandatory, was still a campaign rather than law as of an April 2026 source. — [Open University, updated 21 Apr 2026](https://www.open.edu/openlearn/health-sports-psychology/owens-law-the-case-written-allergen-information-every-food-business). The FSA board said in Dec 2023 that written allergen information should become a legal requirement. — [New Food Magazine](https://newfoodmagazine.com/?p=197015)

**EU (Regulation 1169/2011)**
- Allergen information for non-prepacked food, including in restaurants, has been mandatory since 13 Dec 2014. Article 44 lets member states set how it is delivered. Ireland (S.I. 489/2014, updated by S.I. 656/2024) requires a legible written or printed format that identifies the specific dish, available before purchase without the customer having to ask. Symbols or numbers on the menu are allowed. — [FSAI](https://www.fsai.ie/business-advice/running-a-food-business/allergens/allergen-information-for-non-prepacked-food)
- A vendor blog claims the rules apply whether the menu is printed or digital (commercial claim, not a legal ruling). — [Doxmenu (vendor)](https://doxmenu.com/blog/qr-menu-allergen-compliance-guide/)

**US (FDA)**
- Chains with 20+ locations operating under the same name with substantially the same menu items have had to comply since 7 May 2018. They must show calories on menus and menu boards, a 2,000-calorie statement, and a statement that written nutrition information (fat, saturated and trans fat, cholesterol, sodium, carbs, sugars, fibre, protein) is available on request. The FDA page does not mention allergens or online menus. — [FDA](https://www.fda.gov/food/nutrition-food-labeling-and-critical-foods/menu-labeling-requirements)

**Australia (state laws)**
- Kilojoule menu-labelling laws: NSW (2011), ACT (2012), SA (2012), Queensland (2017) and Victoria (2018, per a 2020 table). — [Nutrients journal table, 2020](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7352298/table/nutrients-12-01544-t003). They generally apply to chains with 20+ outlets in a state or 50+ nationally. As of 2021, WA, Tasmania and NT were voluntary only. — [Dietitians Australia, 2021](https://dietitiansaustralia.org.au/about-us/media-centre/nation-wide-kilojoule-labelling-needed-fast-food-media-release); [HWL Ebsworth](https://hwlebsworth.com.au/labelling-rules-and-kilojoules/)
- Penalties were reported (2018) at up to AUD 3,000 for individuals and AUD 15,000 for corporations. — [Australian Food News](https://www.ausfoodnews.com.au/?p=36998)

**India (home market, for comparison): FSSAI**
- Under the FSSAI Labelling and Display Regulations (notified 2020, effective 1 Jan 2022), food service establishments with a central licence or outlets at 10+ locations must show calories per serving and serving size on menus, boards or booklets, plus a 2,000 kcal reference statement. Sampling to verify claims was paused until 30 Jun 2022. — [S.S. Rana & Co.](https://ssrana.in/articles/display-calorie-values-menus-fssai/); [FSSAI notice, 2020](https://mail.fssai.gov.in/upload/media/FSSAI_News_Outlets_FNB_08_09_2020.pdf)
- Secondary sources say the rules also require allergen information and veg/non-veg logos on menus, with nutrition information available on request. — [Food Safety Helpline](https://foodsafetyhelpline.com/display-of-information-in-food-service-establishment/)

**Do digital (QR) menus count?**
- Saudi Arabia: digital menus and delivery apps are explicitly in scope (2019: "phone and web applications"; 2025: "physical and digital menus, including food delivery apps"). — [The National](https://www.thenationalnews.com/uae/health/saudi-arabia-brings-in-mandatory-calorie-labels-on-menus-1.808556); [Gulf News](https://gulfnews.com/amp/story/world%2Fgulf%2Fsaudi%2Fsaudi-arabia-enforces-new-menu-rules-to-flag-salt-caffeine-and-calorie-burn-1.500183654)
- UK: online and app menus are "menus", and information is needed at every point of choice. If a QR menu is where the guest chooses, it must carry calories. Whether a QR-only menu can replace a printed one is not addressed. — [GOV.UK](https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/calorie-labelling-in-the-out-of-home-sector/calorie-labelling-in-the-out-of-home-sector-implementation-guidance)
- Singapore: Nutri-Grade explicitly covers digital platforms. — [MOH](https://www.moh.gov.sg/newsroom/extension-of-nutri-grade-requirements-to-freshly-prepared-beverages-from-30-december-2023/)
- Ireland: requires written or printed information available without asking. Whether a QR code meets this is not settled in the sources found. — [FSAI](https://www.fsai.ie/business-advice/running-a-food-business/allergens/allergen-information-for-non-prepacked-food)

### Inferences
- Saudi Arabia is the clearest "compliance pull" market. All F&B establishments (no size threshold found) must now show calories, allergens, sodium, caffeine and activity equivalents on every menu format, including digital. Producing this data per dish is costly for operators, and DineView's nutrition-first menu with tap-to-see calories, macros and allergens maps directly onto it. The 2023 study showing 43% non-compliance on delivery apps suggests many operators still lack tooling.
- In the UK, US, Australia and India, the calorie mandates mostly hit large chains: enterprise accounts with long sales cycles and existing POS/menu vendors. The UK's push on written allergen information (FSA 2025 guidance plus the Owen's Law campaign) is a "coming regulation" story, not a current mandate.
- In Dubai and Abu Dhabi the case has to rest on guest experience, voluntary health programmes (Dubai's healthy logo, Abu Dhabi's SEHHI) and the 2026 Abu Dhabi study showing calorie labels reduce ordered calories. It cannot rest on legal compulsion.
- The safest product stance is to put calories and allergens directly on the item card (not a second tap away). The digital menu then meets "next to the item / at point of choice" wording in the UK and Saudi Arabia. Also offer a printable or exportable compliant menu for restaurants that cannot rely on QR alone.
- DineView can also sell the FSSAI rule (10+ outlets or central licence) to Indian chains in Bengaluru and the other metros, which builds the nutrition-data pipeline before going abroad.

### Gaps
- SFDA's own decree text, number(s), penalty schedule and any size threshold were not retrieved: the Mwasfah portal and SFDA site were not fetched, and the details come from Gulf News and vendor summaries. Allergen-rule effective date conflicts: 1 Jan 2025 vs 1 Jul 2025.
- No 2025–2026 Dubai Municipality statement was found either reinstating or formally abandoning the 2019 calorie mandate.
- The date of Qatar's franchise-calorie circular and its enforcement status were not found.
- No regulator explicitly ruling on QR-only menus was found in any market.
- Natasha's Law (UK full-ingredient labelling for food prepacked for direct sale, generally cited as in force from 1 Oct 2021) was not re-verified here. It mainly affects grab-and-go, not table-service menus.
- FDA's treatment of online menus and the current status of Queensland and Victoria kJ schemes were not verified against primary pages (the NSW Food Authority page timed out).
- Other markets (Canada/Ontario, Kuwait, Bahrain, Oman, Malaysia) were not researched.

## 2. Market sizes, restaurant counts, diaspora, spend and tech/QR adoption

### Takeaway
The UAE foodservice market is about USD 18.6bn (2025), and Dubai alone has about 26,000 licensed food establishments (2024), with about 2,300 new openings in H1 2025. Saudi foodservice is larger, at USD 27–30bn (2024–2025 estimates), and growing about 8% a year. Britain's licensed-premises count is shrinking: 98,914 at end-2025, with restaurants hit hardest. The Indian diaspora is concentrated in the Gulf: about 3.57M in the UAE and about 2.59M in Saudi Arabia per MEA. No reliable per-diner spend figures or independent QR-adoption rates were found for any target market.

### Inferences
- By addressable venues, Dubai (about 26k food establishments, adding more than 4k a year at the 2025 pace) is comparable to a large Indian metro, but at Gulf price points. Saudi Arabia is the largest GCC foodservice market by value and has the compliance mandate.
- The UK is a large market but is contracting and under cost pressure (net closures through 2025). Diners there are split on QR versus physical menus, so a QR-first product faces resistance, especially in fine dining.
- The diaspora angle is strongest in the UAE and Saudi Arabia: about 3.6M and 2.6M Indians, many in hospitality ownership and operations. That gives warm-intro channels and Indian-cuisine restaurants that may relate to the Bengaluru reference customers.

### Gaps

- No Singapore F&B establishment count or market size was retrieved.
- UK and Singapore Indian-diaspora figures were not retrieved from MEA in this pass.
- No independent (non-vendor) 2025–2026 QR-ordering adoption rate was found for any target market.

## 3. Ease of selling from India: setup, payments, data rules, and Indian restaurant-tech players that went abroad

### Takeaway
Dubai is the easiest first overseas base. A free-zone software licence costs roughly AED 12k–25k a year (about AED 20k–50k in year one with visa), but a free-zone entity generally cannot sell directly to mainland restaurants without a mainland branch or local distributor. Saudi Arabia adds friction: PDPL data-localisation expectations, ZATCA e-invoicing in POS flows, and Arabic-first menus. Indian restaurant-tech companies have repeatedly used Dubai as the regional hub: Restroworks (ex-Posist) since 2017 with a DIFC office and later a Riyadh branch, Petpooja targeting MENA from 2021, and UrbanPiper with Gulf enterprise logos and a Saudi push.

### Cited Findings
- Dubai free-zone licences for software and IT run about AED 12,000–18,000 a year. A realistic first year is AED 20,000–30,000 with a flexi-desk and one visa. Mainland software licences are about AED 10,797 plus a 2.5% rent market fee and need a physical office of at least about 200 sq ft (EJARI), with a first-year budget of AED 30,000–50,000. — [vOffice, 2026 guide](https://voffice.co.id/blog/en/dubai-company-setup-cost/) (consultancy source)
- Free-zone companies generally cannot trade directly on the UAE mainland unless they appoint a local distributor or service agent or open a mainland branch. — [Flyingcolour](https://www.flyingcolour.net/blog/opening-a-software-company-in-dubai/) (consultancy source). Software development can be 100% foreign-owned on the mainland, with a UAE national as local service agent. — same source
- Meydan Free Zone publishes a guide aimed at Indian founders on Dubai licence costs. — [Meydan FZ](https://www.meydanfz.ae/relocation-dubai-india/dubai-business-license-cost-for-indians)
- Saudi PDPL is described as requiring data on Saudi residents to be stored and processed in the Kingdom unless regulatory approval permits cross-border transfer, with breach notice to SDAIA within 72 hours. — [Captain Compliance (vendor)](https://captaincompliance.com/education/saudi-arabia-pdpl-compliance-software-a-comprehensive-guide-to-on-premise-data-privacy-solutions/)
- ZATCA e-invoicing applies to B2B, B2C and B2G transactions in phased waves. POS systems must issue ZATCA-compliant simplified e-invoices. — [POSbytz](https://posbytz.com/exploring-e-invoicing-in-saudi-arabia/)
- **Restroworks (ex-Posist):** says it expanded into the Middle East in 2017, has a UAE office in DIFC, and deployed 25,000+ restaurants in 2025 with USD 5.57bn in orders processed (self-reported, no regional split). — [Restroworks official info](https://www.restroworks.com/llm-info/). The 2024 rebrand release names offices in Dubai, Riyadh and Singapore, reports 20,000 customers in 50+ countries and 80% YoY growth, and cites five years of investment in the US, Middle East, LatAm and SEA. — [CXOToday, 2024](https://cxotoday.com/press-release/posist-transforms-into-restroworks-unveiling-a-unified-restaurant-technology-platform/)
- **Petpooja:** its 2021 round (about USD 4.5M, led by Aroa Ventures) was partly to expand in the Middle East and Africa. It was then running at 25,000+ outlets across India, the UAE and Africa. — [YourStory, Nov 2021](https://yourstory.com/2021/11/point-of-sale-fnb-petpooja-funding-udaan); [Inc42](https://inc42.com/?p=268825)
- **UrbanPiper:** in 2022 it listed Gulf enterprise customers including Americana Group, McDonald's, Krispy Kreme and KFC, and integrates with Saudi POS Foodics. — [UrbanPiper blog, 2022](https://urbanpiper.com/blog/urbanpiper-makes-a-mark-at-the-fast-food-cafe-convention-2022). It also scaled a partnership with HB Brands as a route into Saudi Arabia. — [UrbanPiper blog](https://urbanpiper.com/blog/urbanpiper-looks-set-to-continue-scaling-hb-brands-in-saudi-arabia)
- **peAR (Mumbai):** an Indian 3D/AR restaurant-menu startup that raised seed funding from Venture Catalysts. No Gulf presence was found. — [Inc42](https://inc42.com/?p=176801)

### Inferences
- The proven Indian playbook is: (1) a Dubai entity (DIFC or a free zone) as regional HQ, (2) land multi-country GCC restaurant groups (Americana, HB Brands-type franchisors) that then pull the product into Saudi Arabia, Qatar and Kuwait, and (3) integrate with the dominant local POS (Foodics in Saudi Arabia) rather than replace it. UrbanPiper's Americana/McDonald's/KFC logos and its Foodics integration show this route works for a menu or ordering layer.
- To sell directly to Dubai restaurants, DineView needs either a mainland licence or a free-zone entity plus a mainland agent/branch. That choice should be made before the first UAE invoice.
- For Saudi Arabia, plan in-Kingdom (or approved) hosting for guest and order data, Arabic/RTL menus, and pass-through to ZATCA-compliant POS (DineView should not issue tax invoices itself if the POS does). Each of these is engineering work that UAE and India don't need.
- DineView's per-outlet SaaS plus capture-fee model suits Gulf groups that buy centrally for many outlets.

### Gaps
- Official DET/free-zone fee schedules were not retrieved (consultancy figures only).
- SDAIA's PDPL text on processor obligations and offshore hosting for foreign SaaS was not verified. Whether Saudi Arabia requires a MISA licence or local entity to sell SaaS cross-border was not researched.
- Payment rails for collecting SaaS fees from the Gulf to India (e.g., Stripe/Checkout availability, RBI/FEMA export-of-services rules) were not researched.
- UAE federal PDPL implications for guest data were not researched.
- No first-hand "lessons learned" interviews or articles from Restroworks, Petpooja or UrbanPiper on what went wrong or right abroad were found. Their Middle East revenue shares are not public in the sources found.

## 4. Competitors in target markets (QR ordering, pay-at-table, AR menus) and what they charge

The Gulf QR space is dominated by pay-at-table fintechs. ## 5. Recommended expansion sequence and reasoning

### Takeaway
The evidence supports: Bengaluru → Mumbai / Delhi NCR / Hyderabad / Pune (use FSSAI's chain calorie rule) → Dubai as first overseas base (easy setup, 26k food establishments, 3.6M Indian diaspora, regional HQ for GCC groups) → Riyadh/Jeddah as the main compliance-driven market (SFDA rules since 1 Jul 2025) → then opportunistically Qatar and Kuwait via GCC groups. The UK (London, 250+ employee chains, pending written-allergen reform) and Singapore (drinks-only rule, cheap competitors) come later. The US and Australia are lower priority: chain-only rules and entrenched local competitors.

### Cited Findings
- **India metros:** the FSSAI calorie display rule covers establishments with a central licence or 10+ outlets (effective 1 Jan 2022). — [S.S. Rana](https://ssrana.in/articles/display-calorie-values-menus-fssai/)
- **Dubai:** 26,000 food establishments (2024) and 2,336 new outlets in H1 2025. — [WAM](https://www.wam.ae/en/article/b1qrfvc-over-26000-food-establishments-operate-dubai); [Dubai Eye](https://www.dubaieye1038.com/news/local/dubai-steps-up-food-safety-with-over-34000-inspections/). The calorie mandate is voluntary since its deferral. — [Gulf News](https://gulfnews.com/uae/calorie-display-in-dubai-menus-delayed-for-two-years-1.65352261). Indian diaspora about 3.57M. — [MEA](https://www.mea.gov.in/population-of-overseas-indians.htm). Restroworks' regional office is in DIFC. — [Restroworks](https://www.restroworks.com/llm-info/)
- **Saudi Arabia:** mandatory calories, allergens, sodium, caffeine and activity labels on all menu formats, including digital and delivery apps, since 1 Jul 2025. — [Gulf News](https://gulfnews.com/amp/story/world%2Fgulf%2Fsaudi%2Fsaudi-arabia-enforces-new-menu-rules-to-flag-salt-caffeine-and-calorie-burn-1.500183654); [Foodics](https://www.foodics.com/calorie-counts-on-menus-new-sfda-regulations-for-restaurants/). Market about USD 30bn, growing about 8.2% CAGR to 2030. — [Gulfbase](https://www.gulfbase.com/news/saudi-arabia-s-food-services-market-expected-to-reach-45bn-by-2030/213093). Indian diaspora about 2.59M. — [MEA](https://www.mea.gov.in/population-of-overseas-indians.htm)
- **UK:** calorie law only for 250+ employee businesses. — [GOV.UK](https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/calorie-labelling-in-the-out-of-home-sector/calorie-labelling-in-the-out-of-home-sector-implementation-guidance). Written allergen information is recommended, not required, and Owen's Law is still a campaign. — [FSA](https://www.food.gov.uk/business-guidance/allergen-information-for-non-prepacked-foods-best-practice-introduction); [Open University](https://www.open.edu/openlearn/health-sports-psychology/owens-law-the-case-written-allergen-information-every-food-business). Venue count is falling. — [NIQ](https://nielseniq.com/global/en/news-center/2026/hospitality-closures-accelerate-in-fourth-quarter-as-costs-rise-again/)
- **Singapore:** only freshly prepared drinks are covered by Nutri-Grade. — [MOH](https://www.moh.gov.sg/newsroom/extension-of-nutri-grade-requirements-to-freshly-prepared-beverages-from-30-december-2023/). QR ordering sells at about SGD 12 per outlet per month (grant package). — [GoBusiness](https://www.gobusiness.gov.sg/images/psg/TabSquare_Smartweb_VersionV3_Desensitised_Annex3_Part5.pdf)
- **Competitive timing:** Sunday plans UAE entry in 2026. — [Sifted](https://sifted.eu/articles/restaurant-fintech-sunday-21m-fundraise). Qlub is already in the UAE, KSA, Qatar and Kuwait. — [Wamda](https://www.wamda.com/2025/07/qlub-closes-30-million-round-backed-shorooq-e-mubadala)

### Inferences
- **Step 1, India metros (now to about 12 months):** prove the product, the 3D-capture operations and unit economics. Target chains with 10+ outlets that must comply with FSSAI calorie display, and use the nutrition data pipeline as the wedge. Build Arabic/RTL support and per-market regulatory templates (SFDA, UK, FSSAI) in parallel.
- **Step 2, Dubai (first overseas):** cheapest, fastest entity (free zone or DIFC plus a mainland route for direct sales), and the most Indian-owned or Indian-run F&B groups outside India. Gulf price points sit in a market of about 26k establishments that is still growing. Lead with guest experience (AR, swipe menu) and voluntary health positioning: Dubai's healthy logo, the Abu Dhabi SEHHI programme, and the July 2026 Abu Dhabi study showing calorie labels cut about 114 kcal per order. Land GCC multi-country groups headquartered in Dubai. Move before Sunday's 2026 UAE launch matures, and partner with rather than fight Qlub on payments.
- **Step 3, Riyadh/Jeddah:** the biggest pull, because every restaurant and cafe must show calories, allergens, sodium, caffeine and activity equivalents on digital and print menus. DineView can sell "SFDA-compliant digital menu out of the box", plus AR as the upsell. Entry cost is higher: PDPL hosting, a ZATCA-aware POS integration (Foodics), and Arabic. Enter via GCC groups already won in Dubai, and through a Foodics integration or marketplace listing.
- **Step 4, optional GCC follow-ons (Qatar, Kuwait):** via the same restaurant groups. Qatar's calorie requirement appears limited to franchise chains.
- **Step 5, UK (London) or Singapore:** the UK suits enterprise chain deals (250+ employees) once DineView has compliance references, and could become urgent if Owen's Law or an FSA mandate for written allergen information passes. The UK market is shrinking and diners are split on QR menus. Singapore has limited regulatory pull (drinks only) and low price points. The US and Australia rank last: chain-only rules, entrenched QR players (me&u/Mr Yum in Australia), and high go-to-market cost.
- The main risk to this sequence is that the Saudi regulatory wedge relies on news and vendor summaries. Confirm the SFDA decree text, size thresholds, penalty schedule and whether QR-only digital menus satisfy the "menu" requirement before committing.

### Gaps
- No primary data on willingness to pay or price benchmarks for menu SaaS in the Gulf to size DineView's per-outlet price or capture fee.
- No evidence was found on how strictly SFDA is enforcing the July 2025 rules in 2026 (inspection counts, fines).
- No data on the share of Dubai or Riyadh restaurants owned or operated by Indian groups (the diaspora channel is an inference from population figures).
