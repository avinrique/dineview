# Competitive landscape and pricing: QR ordering, digital menus and AR/3D food menus (India and global), as of Oct 2026

Scope note: research done 27 Oct 2026. Where a figure is older, the year is given next to it. "Vendor claim" means the number comes from the company selling the product, or from its marketing, and has not been independently checked. Currency conversions are not made unless a source gives them.

## 1. Indian restaurant-tech players (QR menus, table ordering, POS, dine-out): offering, pricing, scale, funding, 3D/AR and nutrition

### Takeaway
India's restaurant-tech market is a POS-led oligopoly. Petpooja (100,000+ restaurants claimed) dominates SMB POS, and Restroworks (Posist) and UrbanPiper serve chains. Each already bundles "scan & order" QR ordering, a KDS and waiter calling into the POS, at about ₹20,000/outlet per year (Petpooja). Swiggy Dineout and Zomato District own dine-out discovery and payment, and they make money from commissions and discounts. None of the players reviewed offers 3D/AR dish visualisation. None offers per-dish nutrition or allergen display on the in-restaurant QR menu. The closest is Zomato's "Healthy Mode" on delivery.

### Inferences
- **Price anchor for DineView:** QR ordering + KDS + waiter calling is already a bundled feature of incumbent POS at about ₹20,000–30,000 per outlet per year. This assumes the INR Petpooja prices are annual per outlet, which the annual-only billing and the USD page's "per year/per outlet" wording suggest, though the INR page does not say so. DineView cannot charge a premium for QR ordering/KDS by itself. Any premium has to come from AR/3D, nutrition/allergen data and conversion uplift.
- No Indian POS or dine-out player in this review shows AR/3D dish visualisation. On the in-restaurant QR menu, nutrition is limited to whatever text the restaurant types in. Zomato's Healthy Mode shows that nutrition scoring is a priority for the largest consumer platform, but on delivery, not on table QR.
- Swiggy Dineout and District are not table-ordering products. They monetise discovery, booking and bill payment through commissions and discounts. That makes them adjacent, possibly complementary (a DineView guest still arrives via Dineout), rather than direct competitors. But they hold the diner relationship.
- Thrive's shutdown suggests that a low-commission model that depends on pulling consumers away from aggregators struggles in India. Pure SaaS that does not need consumer acquisition (guests are already at the table) avoids that problem.

### Gaps
- **MenuSutra:** no information found. The domain menusutra.com did not resolve, and searches returned only similarly named products (MenuSifu, MenuPro). Its existence and status are unconfirmed.
- The Petpooja INR pricing page does not state the billing basis (per outlet / per year). This needs a sales call to confirm.
- No current (2025–26) outlet counts for DotPe/Rista, GoFrugal ServeEasy or TMBill. Restroworks' funding history is contradictory. No 2026 Dineout or District commission rate was found (latest: 4–10%, 2022).
- Rista, GoFrugal, TMBill and LimeTray: no evidence either way on AR or nutrition display (nothing found). The absence was not exhaustively verified.

## 2. APIs and integrations a QR-ordering layer could plug into (partnership vs competition)

### Takeaway
Restroworks advertises open APIs and a 500+ integration marketplace. Petpooja has a partner order-push / menu-sync API (app key + secret + access token per restaurant) and a 150+ app marketplace, but its official developer docs are not public, and one reviewer says it lacks open APIs. Incumbents already sell their own QR ordering, so DineView would plug into a POS whose vendor competes with it for the same ₹20k bundle.

### Cited Findings
- Petpooja claims 150+ third-party integrations (a listing says 200+). Named integrations include Swiggy, Zomato, Dineout, Paytm and Tally. QR ordering is built in, not a partner product — [Petpooja app marketplace](https://www.petpooja.com/poss/restaurant-app-marketplace); [Petpooja online ordering](https://www.petpooja.com/online-ordering)
- An unofficial, community-maintained OpenAPI spec for Petpooja describes: fetching a mapped restaurant's menu, receiving full-menu pushes on a partner "Push Menu" endpoint, pushing online orders into the POS (`save_order`), order-status callbacks to a partner `callback_url`, and item stock / store on-off. Auth is app_key + app_secret + access_token scoped to a restID. Some endpoints are "not fully published" — [apis.io – Petpooja Orders API](https://apis.io/apis/petpooja/petpooja-orders-api/)
- A live third-party integration (Yelo by Jungleworks) syncs categories and products and pushes orders with status updates into Petpooja. Add-ons and taxes in menu sync are **not** supported — [Jungleworks help](https://help.jungleworks.com/yelo/petpooja-integration/)
- A GetApp reviewer wrote: "I haven't seen Open APIs. Limited integrations with 3rd party software." — [GetApp reviews](https://www.getapp.com/all-software/a/petpooja/reviews/)
- Petpooja's Operations Manager tiers list "API" as an included feature — [Petpooja pricing](https://www.petpooja.com/pricing)
- Restroworks: "Open APIs … Connect your stack, build what you need", a "500+ native integrations" marketplace, and "Neo" integration middleware for online orders. It also sells its own QR ordering (RestroScan) — [Restroworks](https://www.restroworks.com/?p=37818)
- UrbanPiper's core business is integration middleware (aggregator order routing). Swiggy and Zomato are investors (2022) — [Inc42](https://inc42.com/?p=285324)
- DotPe built its dine-in QR ordering and Rista POS in-house — [YourStory](https://yourstory.com/2021/09/o2o-commerce-platform-dotpe-enters-pos-space-with-rista-acquisition)
- Petpooja's own AR glossary frames AR as a "visual add-on" that relies on the POS's centralised menu, pricing, availability and KOT routing. The POS leader is effectively describing an AR layer on top of its stack, not one it builds — [Petpooja AR glossary](https://blog.petpooja.com/glossary/ar-menu/)

### Inferences
- **Partnership route:** DineView as an "AR + nutrition menu layer" that pulls the menu from Petpooja/Restroworks and pushes orders and KOTs back via their partner APIs. This avoids rip-and-replace, which matters because Petpooja is in 100k+ outlets. The Yelo case suggests modifier and tax mapping will be the hard part.
- **Competition risk:** Petpooja and Restroworks bundle QR ordering, KDS and waiter calls. DineView's own KDS, waiter calls and admin overlap with what POS-equipped outlets already pay for. A POS-integrated "menu experience" SKU, priced below or alongside the POS add-on, is likely easier to sell than a parallel ordering stack. This needs validation with restaurant owners.
- Petpooja's Android POS does not support Scan & Order. Outlets on that POS, and outlets with no POS, are a possible wedge for a standalone DineView ordering stack.

### Gaps
- Official Petpooja partner documentation, API access terms and fees, and whether Petpooja permits third-party dine-in (table) orders through the same `save_order` flow, were not found. Contacting the Petpooja partnerships team is required.
- Restroworks API pricing and partner terms are not public.

## 3. Global QR / table-ordering reference players: pricing, scale, what happened

### Takeaway
Well-funded global QR order-and-pay startups consolidated or shrank after the pandemic. Mr Yum and me&u merged and cut staff, Sunday exited 60% of its markets, and Bbot was absorbed by DoorDash. POS giants (Toast, Square) now bundle QR ordering cheaply. The standalone QR-ordering layer has proven hard to sustain on its own.

### Cited Findings
- Mr Yum (Australia) raised >$100M, including an $89M Series A in Dec 2021. It cut ~17% of staff, then merged with me&u in an all-stock deal (2023) to become "the world's largest ordering and payments platform for hospitality", targeting profitability in 2024 — [Business News Australia](https://www.businessnewsaustralia.com/articles/hospitality-platforms-mr-yum-and-me-u-merging-to-become-a-new-global-giant.html). me&u later laid off ~10% a year after the merger — [iTiger](https://itiger.com/news/2485220485)
- Sunday (Paris; QR pay-at-table) raised a $24M seed and then a $100M Series A in Sept 2021 led by Coatue — [Restaurant Business](https://www.restaurantbusinessonline.com/technology/restaurant-payment-startup-sunday-raises-100m). It later exited 60% of its markets (Italy, Spain, Canada, Portugal), kept the US, UK and France, and cut staff, citing investor pressure on profitability — [Restaurant Dive](https://www.restaurantdive.com/news/restaurant-payments-app-sunday-cuts-staff-exits-60-of-its-markets/627312)
- Bbot (US; in-venue QR ordering) was acquired by DoorDash in Mar 2022 for undisclosed terms — [Restaurant Dive](https://www.restaurantdive.com/news/doordash-buys-bbot-to-bolster-in-store-digital-ordering-capabilities/619559/). CB Insights lists ~$22M raised — [CB Insights](https://www.cbinsights.com/investor/bbot)
- Toast: Core plan $69/month. Mobile Order & Pay is an optional add-on with no published price — [NerdWallet](https://www.nerdwallet.com/article/small-business/toast-pos-review)
- Square Self-Serve Ordering (QR) is included with Square Online. Square claims businesses see "a 35% increase in sales within the first 30 days" (vendor claim; unclear whether this is ticket size or volume) — [Square](https://squareup.com/us/en/the-bottom-line/reaching-customers/qr-code-ordering-system-ticket-size)

### Inferences
- The global pattern is that QR ordering becomes a commodity POS feature, and standalone players are acquired, merge or retrench. DineView's defensibility has to come from something POS vendors don't bundle (3D/AR content, nutrition data), not from QR ordering itself.

### Gaps
- Current me&u and Sunday pricing and restaurant counts were not retrieved. The exact date of Sunday's retrenchment was not confirmed from the article snippet.

## 4. AR / 3D menu companies worldwide and in India: pricing, traction, fate, lessons

### Takeaway
AR menus have been tried since ~2016 (Kabaq → QReal, Menu3, peAR) and have not scaled. Kabaq/QReal pivoted to social and e-commerce 3D ads and was divested by Glimpse in a 2024 management buyout. India's only funded AR-menu startup, peAR, raised under ₹7 Cr total and has no visible activity after 2023. Practitioners named the causes as app-download friction, the cost and logistics of producing 3D models per dish, and weak measured ROI. New entrants (e.g. UK ARmenu, 2026) now use app-less WebAR (Quick Look / Scene Viewer) plus phone photogrammetry (Apple Object Capture), at about £49 per dish.

### Cited Findings

**Kabaq → QReal (New York; Glimpse Group)**
- The Glimpse Group acquired KabaQ's assets on 20 Dec 2016 and formed KabaQ 3D Food Technologies LLC — [GlobeNewswire](https://www.globenewswire.com/fr/news-release/2016/12/20/1060624/0/en/The-Glimpse-Group-Acquires-the-Assets-of-KabaQ-and-Forms-KabaQ-3D-Food-Technologies-LLC.html)
- 2017: charged restaurants **$99–$199/month**. Staff photographed dishes on a portable turntable photo booth and built the models at the office. One owner (Vino Levantino, NYC) said overall sales rose ~22% after adopting it (single-owner anecdote) — [VOA, 25 Nov 2017](https://www.voanews.com/a/augmented-reality-3-d-food-models-on-the-menu/4136235.html). - Restaurants shot a series of photos per dish (~10–20 minutes), and the team built a 3D model in 1–2 days. It was free for restaurants to join at the time — [Built In Chicago, 2018](https://www.builtinchicago.org/articles/menu3-augmented-reality-food-app). Current status not found.

CB Insights: last raise $510K on 16 Mar 2023 — [CB Insights](https://www.cbinsights.com/compare/menu3-vs-pear-technologies)
- Claimed traction (company claims): 800 restaurant partners and 35k orders/month — [Entrackr](https://entrackr.com/?p=165720). A 3D model "in under 40 minutes" via an image-to-3D pipeline, and "increase their profits by upto 20%" — [Verdict Foodservice](https://verdictfoodservice.com/?p=35067)
- Its pitch framed dine-in as ~$50B against ~$10B for delivery in India — [Verdict Foodservice](https://www.verdictfoodservice.com/news/pear-funding-ipv/)

- From £49 per dish. Growth package £149 for 5 dishes, Pro £299 for 15. Prices include 3D creation, CDN hosting and print-ready QR PDFs. Restaurants send 20–30 photos per dish, and the model is built with **Apple Object Capture**, with QR codes delivered in ~48 hours. App-less: iPhone AR Quick Look / Android Scene Viewer. **Others**

- "ARitize3D" turned out to be a Shopify product-3D app at $9.99 per SKU, not a restaurant menu product — [Digismoothie](https://www.digismoothie.com/ja/app/aritize3d)

- India status (vendor view): AR menus are "early-stage tech, spotted at flagship outlets in Indiranagar, Bangalore or a high-end lounge in Bandra", and "verified Indian data" was "limited" as of June 2026 — [Petpooja AR glossary](https://blog.petpooja.com/glossary/ar-menu/)

### Inferences (lessons from failures)
1. **App-download friction killed the first wave.** Kabaq relied on Snapchat lenses and apps, and peAR on a native app. DineView's no-install WebAR via QR answers the main barrier QReal named.
2. **Content production is the bottleneck.** Kabaq sent staff with turntable rigs, and Menu3 took 1–2 days per model. Per-dish capture cost and turnaround (menus change and dishes rotate) decide unit economics. Phone-based photogrammetry (Object Capture, ~48h turnaround at ARmenu) changes the cost curve.
3. **ROI was never independently proven at scale.** All sales-lift numbers (22%, 20–26%, 2.4×) are single-owner or vendor-reported. Funders and restaurants stayed sceptical, and QReal drifted to brand/e-commerce work, where budgets exist.
4. **Standalone AR without ordering and POS integration had no hook into daily operations.** peAR did combine AR with ordering, but on a native app with a commission model, and its funding was small (<₹7 Cr).
5. **Business model:** consumer-app plus commission (peAR) and brand-marketing (Bareburger/Snapchat) models did not sustain. A per-outlet SaaS plus capture fee (ARmenu, Kabaq's $99–199/mo) is the model still in market.

### Gaps
- **Arvolution, Foodar and Yummy AR:** no information found in searches. Existence, pricing and status are unverified.
- No information on whether QReal still sells restaurant menus after the buyout, or on peAR's 2024–26 status.
- Kabaq's peak restaurant count is unclear (15 in 2017 versus a vendor-repeated "800+").

## 5. Evidence that 3D/AR or richer digital menus lift orders, AOV or upsell

### Takeaway
Peer-reviewed evidence shows that AR menus raise visit intention, engagement, perceived learning and purchase intention for served food. No peer-reviewed field study with audited sales was found. The only measured-behaviour AR study (Oxford with QReal, 2019) reported dessert ordering more than doubling, but it was co-run by the vendor and never fully published. One study found AR *reduces* over-ordering by making portion size clearer. QR-ordering AOV lifts of 20–60% are vendor claims, and digital-menu-board check lifts are smaller (3–9%). A 2026 Indian RCT found calorie labels cut calories ordered by 10–12% at one chain, relevant to "nutrition-first".

### Cited Findings

**Academic / independent**
- **Seo, Han & Howlett, *International Journal of Hospitality Management* (2026):** two experiments compared printed, QR and AR menus (the AR version showed a 3D dish with tappable ingredient sourcing). AR raised **intention to visit** and **likelihood of sharing (WOM)**, plus immersion and perceived learning. The effect was larger for a lower-health-image brand (McDonald's vs Panera) and depended on "strategic fit". It measured intentions, not orders — [WSU press release, 18 Feb 2026](https://news.wsu.edu/press-release/2026/02/18/augmented-reality-menus-may-help-restaurants-attract-customers/)
- **Petit, Javornik & Velasco, *Journal of Retailing* (2021, in press at the time):** three studies. For **served food, AR (versus 3D on screen) improved mental simulation of eating, which increased purchase intention**, and people were willing to pay more for served food in AR than for packaged food — [BI Business Review, Aug 2021](https://www.bi.no/en/research/business-review/articles/2021/08/we-eat-first-with-our-digital-eyes/)
- **Sharma, Singh & Prasad, *Current Issues in Tourism* (2023):** two experiments, 858 participants in Fiji. AR menus (versus picture menus) **reduced over-ordering intent** through better portion-size perception and food-waste awareness. The effect was stronger for at-home than at-restaurant ordering — [USP repository](https://repository.usp.ac.fj/id/eprint/14342/)
- Online experiment (Portugal, n=426): AR improved sensory perception of dishes, but the authors could not draw conclusions on purchase intention — [ULisboa repository](https://repositorio.ulisboa.pt/entities/publication/57793e22-bb6f-4544-8133-bee1d8533e93)
- **Oxford Saïd Business School with QReal (2019, reported 2020):** dessert ordering was **41.2% with an AR menu versus 18% with a traditional menu**. Average dessert spend was $2.93 versus $1.38 (+112%), and the effect held across age, sex and AR familiarity. Sample size was not reported, only press highlights were shared, and the study was co-run with the AR vendor. The article says it "does not prove AR menus will guarantee higher sales" — [The Spoon, 11 Aug 2020](https://thespoon.tech/as-menus-move-to-mobile-phones-research-shows-ar-could-drive-more-sales/)
- TU Delft MSc thesis (2019): a field experiment with a commercial AR app in a Dutch restaurant on engagement and order value. The results could not be retrieved (server refused connection) — [TU Delft repository](https://repository.tudelft.nl/file/File_a42f5fe0-d9a1-429e-b1f9-3316805bcac5?preview=1)
- Tablet menus: QSR and midscale diners are more likely to adopt tablet menus than upscale diners — [IJCHM 2018/19](https://emerald.com/insight/content/doi/10.1108/IJCHM-04-2018-0307/full/html)
- **ICMR RCT, Chennai, published in *Appetite*, Aug 2026:** 2,462 diners at two chains (7 outlets). At the multi-cuisine chain, icon-based nutrition labels led to **12% fewer calories** in hypothetical orders (daily-intake labels 11%, numeric 10%) and 6% less fat. There was no significant effect at the vegetarian chain. Spend and sales were not measured — [The South First, 18 Aug 2026](https://thesouthfirst.com/health/do-menu-labels-make-diners-choose-fewer-calories-icmr-trial-in-chennai-says-yes-by-12/)

**Vendor claims: QR / table ordering**
- Mr Yum: "average order size increase by 20-40%" (CEO, attributed to photos plus cross-sell and upsell) — [Hospitality Tech](https://hospitalitytech.com/murtec-executive-insight-toolkit-ordering-payments-loyalty-marketing-customer-insights). Lightspeed blog citing Mr Yum data: AOV up 20–60% — [Lightspeed AU](https://www.lightspeedhq.com/au/blog/table-ordering-pros-and-cons/)
- me&u: "customers spending up to 30% more on average" — [Restaurant Online supplier listing](https://www.restaurantonline.co.uk/Suppliers/me-u/)
- Square: +35% sales in the first 30 days after QR self-serve ordering — [Square](https://squareup.com/us/en/the-bottom-line/reaching-customers/qr-code-ordering-system-ticket-size)
- Petpooja: says it "has seen average bill values rise" with scan & order, but gives no percentage — [Petpooja blog](https://blog.petpooja.com/industry-business-guides/what-is-scan-and-order-qr-ordering-indian-restaurants/)

**Vendor claims: AR menus**
- Kabaq network: AOV +20–26% within 90 days, and AR upsell conversion ~3.4x verbal upsell (vendor, unaudited) — [dev.family](https://dev.family/blog/article/augmented-reality-in-restaurants-use-cases-revenue)
- QReal case studies "suggest" AOV +10–20% (as repeated by Petpooja). Petpooja says verified Indian data is limited — [Petpooja AR glossary](https://blog.petpooja.com/glossary/ar-menu/)
- ARmenu: 2.4× order uplift for AR-featured dishes — [Crunchbase](https://www.crunchbase.com/organization/armenu)
- peAR: profits up "upto 20%" — [Verdict Foodservice](https://verdictfoodservice.com/?p=35067)
- A single NYC owner reported sales +22% after Kabaq (2017) — [VOA](https://www.voanews.com/a/augmented-reality-3-d-food-models-on-the-menu/4136235.html)

**Vendor claims: digital menus / boards**
- WAND Digital: +6% average check in its own test across 70+ deli locations — [WAND](https://wanddigital.com/?p=242990)
- eMenu International: average check +3–9% across clients. A Nyack restaurant's 3-month e-menu pilot saw dessert orders up 10% — [RC Business Journal](https://rcbizjournal.com/?p=2953)

### Inferences
- The strongest defensible claim for DineView's pitch is that AR raises engagement, visit and purchase intention, and that it lifted dessert and add-on ordering in one vendor-run study. A guaranteed AOV lift cannot be claimed from independent evidence.
- AR may cut over-ordering (Sharma et al.), which conflicts with an "upsell" pitch but supports a "fewer returns/complaints, less food waste, trust" pitch. This also fits the nutrition-first positioning.
- The ICMR RCT gives local, current evidence that Indian diners respond to calorie labels, though the effect depends on the menu. That supports nutrition display as a diner-value feature, not necessarily a revenue feature.
- Running its own A/B pilot (AR on versus off, per dish and per table) in Bengaluru would give DineView the independent data that every prior AR vendor lacked.

### Gaps
- No peer-reviewed field study with real restaurant sales data on AR menus was found. The Delft thesis results could not be retrieved.
- A Petpooja claim of an "8–12% AOV rise for scan-and-order outlets in late 2025" surfaced in a search summary but **could not be verified** on any Petpooja page. It is excluded from the findings.
- No India-specific measured data on QR-ordering AOV lift was found.

## 6. How competitors price 3D dish capture and how they produce models

### Takeaway
Per-dish 3D capture is priced at about Rs 1,500–4,000 per dish in India (vendor quotes compiled by Petpooja, early 2026) and from £49 per dish in the UK (ARmenu, 2026). Kabaq historically used a monthly fee ($99–199). Production has moved from studio turntable photogrammetry (Kabaq, 2017) and photo series with a 1–2 day turnaround (Menu3, 2018) to restaurant-shot photos processed with Apple Object Capture (ARmenu) and in-house image-to-3D pipelines (peAR, <40 minutes per model claimed).

### Cited Findings
- **India:** 3D modelling Rs 1,500–4,000 per dish ("Indian vendor quotes as of early 2026"). Table stickers or standees Rs 80–150 per table. AR platform subscription Rs 40,000–60,000/year. First-year total Rs 70,000–1,20,000 for 10–15 modelled hero dishes. It used RealityCapture photogrammetry — [Capturing Reality](https://capturingreality.com/Article-KabaQ-Future-Of-Food-Ordering)

- An Indian per-dish capture price of roughly ₹1,500–4,000, or a hero-dish bundle of 10–15 dishes, is the comparable market reference. Selling only hero dishes keeps first-year spend near Petpooja's illustrative ₹70k–1.2L.
- Restaurant-shot photos with automated Object Capture-style processing (ARmenu's model) imply low marginal cost per dish. On-site capture visits (Kabaq's model) were costly. A hybrid approach, with on-site capture at onboarding and restaurant self-capture for menu changes, may suit Indian price points. This needs cost validation.
- Food is glossy, translucent and steaming, which is hard for photogrammetry. No source quantified failure rates for food capture.

### Gaps
- No published per-dish price list was found from any Indian vendor by name. The Rs 1,500–4,000 range is Petpooja's compilation of unnamed vendor quotes.
- No data on how often models must be redone (seasonal menus, plating changes), or on file-size and performance budgets on low-end Android.
- Apple Object Capture pricing and terms were not researched (generally free on Apple devices, but not sourced here).

## 7. What Indian restaurant owners and diners dislike about QR ordering / QR menus

### Takeaway
Global diner surveys are strongly negative on QR *menus*: 81% prefer physical menus in a Sept 2024 Toast survey, and 65% of over-60s are not comfortable with QR codes. In India, the documented frictions are older diners, weak connectivity (basements), loss of server-led upselling in fine dining, accidental or fraudulent orders, and the need to keep staff anyway. Owner anger in India is aimed mainly at aggregator commissions and discounting (8–28% commissions; Bengaluru boycott threat, Jul 2026), which makes commission-free tools appealing. No first-hand Indian Reddit or forum complaint threads could be retrieved.

### Cited Findings

**Indian owner and operator issues**
- Scan & order limitations (Petpooja, Jun 2026): some guests lack smartphones or can't scan, and tier-2/3 walk-ins prefer staff. Connectivity fails in basements (e.g. Lower Parel) and outlet Wi-Fi needs upkeep. At least one waiter is still needed, and cutting staff hurts service. In fine dining it removes the captain's guidance and upselling. Outside scanners or children can create unwanted orders, so staff validation is needed — [Petpooja blog](https://blog.petpooja.com/industry-business-guides/what-is-scan-and-order-qr-ordering-indian-restaurants/)
- In a Lucknow family-restaurant example, 40% of guests are over 55. QR-only creates friction, so the recommendation is a hybrid setup with 3–5 printed backups — [Petpooja blog](https://blog.petpooja.com/industry-business-guides/qr-codes-replacing-physical-menus-restaurants/)
- POS reviews: one owner's customers complained that billing SMS came from "ppooja" (off-brand), and adding variations is confusing — [Capterra](https://www.capterra.com/p/172163/Petpooja-Restaurant-Management-Platform/reviews/?page=2). One reviewer reported no open APIs and limited integrations — [GetApp](https://www.getapp.com/all-software/a/petpooja/reviews/). A G2 comparison summary reports slow performance on menu management and bulk edits, and complicated inventory (aggregated review summary) — [G2](https://www.g2.com/products/harbortouch-pos/reviews)
- A competitor blog alleges Petpooja support delays, cost increases, hardware lock-in and annual contracts. It is unsourced and comes from a competitor — [DineOpen](https://www.dineopen.com/blog/petpooja-alternative-free-2026.html)
- **Aggregator pain (Bengaluru, Jul 2026):** the Bruhat Bengaluru Hotels Association (president S. Subramanya Holla) threatened a Swiggy/Zomato boycott from 15 Aug with 1,000+ eateries. It cited commissions of 8–28% and menu-price inflation "as much as 43 percent" to recover them, and demanded consent before discounts, transparency on ad and payment-gateway fees, and compensation for cancelled orders — [Free Press Journal, 30 Jul 2026](https://www.freepressjournal.in/tech/bengaluru-restaurants-threaten-to-boycott-swiggy-zomato-from-august-15-over-high-commissions)
- **Dine-out discounting (2022):** NRAI said 400+ brands and 900+ outlets in 13 cities sent Swiggy Dineout delisting notices over deep discounts (up to 30% of the bill) and 4–10% commissions — [Inc42, Oct 2022](https://inc42.com/?p=364825)

**Diner attitudes (mostly US data; no India-specific survey found)**
- Toast blind survey (Sept 2024, 850 US adults): 81% prefer a physical menu, 1% prefer QR — [Duck-Hub compilation](https://www.duck-hub.com/blog/qr-code-menu-statistics)
- Ipsos (Jul 2025, n=1,083 US adults): 65% have used a QR menu (up from 42% in Aug 2022). Among QR-menu users, 58% would like to return to paper (51% in 2023) — [Duck-Hub compilation](https://www.duck-hub.com/blog/qr-code-menu-statistics)
- William Blair (Apr 2023): 65% of people aged 60+ are not comfortable using QR to view, order or pay — [Duck-Hub compilation](https://www.duck-hub.com/blog/qr-code-menu-statistics)
- US Foods Diner Dispatch 2024: 90% of Gen Z prefer paper menus over QR, up from 69% in 2023 — [Business Wire](https://www.businesswire.com/news/home/20241008659223/en/US-Foods-2024-Diner-Dispatch-Survey-Uncovers-Surprising-Shifts-in-Diner-Preferences)
- The frequently repeated "88% prefer paper" (Technomic/WSJ) figure has no publicly accessible methodology — [Duck-Hub compilation](https://www.duck-hub.com/blog/qr-code-menu-statistics)
- Common complaints: codes that force app downloads, large files with no free Wi-Fi, menus that don't load or aren't mobile-optimised — [Tasting Table](https://www.tastingtable.com/2114356/restaurant-change-boomers-hate-qr-code-menus). QR menus "are almost universally disliked" (Kristen Hawley, Expedite, via NYT/BI) — [Yahoo/BI](https://tech.yahoo.com/business/articles/people-hate-qr-code-menus-164525439.html). Some restaurants are reintroducing paper menus — [CTV Ottawa](https://ottawa.ctvnews.ca/restaurants-reintroducing-paper-menus-amid-qr-code-backlash-1.6913501)

### Inferences
- The global backlash is against QR menus as a PDF-style, worse version of paper. A product that offers something paper can't (real-size 3D dish, instant calories and allergens) addresses the "why should I scan" objection better than a list menu. The evidence for this is the AR engagement and intention studies in §5, not direct survey data.
- Product requirements suggested by the complaints: no app or login, fast load on 4G and patchy basements (lightweight models, progressive loading), a staff-assisted mode and waiter call for older guests, order validation to prevent stray orders, and paper or hybrid fallback.
- The Bengaluru commission fight creates a sales opening for commission-free SaaS that drives *dine-in* spend. The messaging should stay clear of Dineout/District discount economics.

### Gaps
- **No first-hand Indian Reddit, Google review or forum threads** on QR ordering were retrievable. Searches restricted to reddit.com returned no Reddit pages. Indian diner sentiment is undocumented beyond vendor blogs.
- No independent India survey of diners or owners on QR ordering was found.

## 8. Where the gap is for an AR-first, nutrition-first table menu (synthesis, with regulatory context)

### Takeaway
Nobody in India currently combines no-install WebAR dish visualisation, per-dish nutrition/allergen display and table ordering. POS incumbents bundle plain QR ordering. Dine-out aggregators monetise discounts. The one AR-menu startup (peAR) used an app and has gone quiet. FSSAI already requires chains with 10+ outlets (or a central licence) to display kcal per serving on menus, and Zomato is pushing nutrition scoring on delivery. Both point to compliance and health demand that a nutrition-first menu can serve.

### Cited Findings
- FSSAI Labelling & Display amendment: food-service businesses with a **central licence or outlets at 10+ locations must show calories (kcal per serving and serving size)** on menu cards, boards and booklets, plus a 2,000 kcal reference statement. It took effect on 1 Jan 2022, with enforcement sampling deferred to mid-2022 — [S.S. Rana](https://ssrana.in/articles/display-calorie-values-menus-fssai/); [FSSAI guidance note, Feb 2022](https://mail.fssai.gov.in/upload/uploadfiles/files/Guidance_Note_Labelling_23_02_2022.pdf). FSSAI sent notices to at least 16 leading eateries for non-compliance (2022) — [India.com](https://www.india.com/health/fssai-restaurants-eateries-dine-out-must-mention-calories-on-menu-or-shut-down-5622636/)
- Sources disagree on any turnover threshold (₹25 Cr versus ₹50 Cr central-licence references). The 10-location trigger is consistent — [Finshots](https://finshots.in/archive/calorie-count-on-your-menus/); [Petpooja blog](https://blog.petpooja.com/?p=46208)
- Zomato Healthy Mode (Sept 2025) scores dishes on protein, complex carbs, fibre and micronutrients — [Storyboard18](https://www.storyboard18.com/brand-marketing/zomato-launches-healthy-mode-with-ai-powered-nutritional-scoring-starting-in-gurgaon-81724.htm)
- ICMR RCT (2026): nutrition labels shifted Indian diners' choices at one chain — [The South First](https://thesouthfirst.com/health/do-menu-labels-make-diners-choose-fewer-calories-icmr-trial-in-chennai-says-yes-by-12/)
- Petpooja positions AR as an add-on it does not build — [Petpooja AR glossary](https://blog.petpooja.com/glossary/ar-menu/)
- Swiggy Dineout has 59k active restaurant partners and is growing ~45% YoY. This shows the size of organised, tech-adopting dine-in restaurants in India — [Swiggy shareholder letter](https://nsearchives.nseindia.com/corporate/SWIGGY_30072026154100_Covering_Letter_Letter_to_Shareholders_-_signed.pdf)

### Inferences
- **Gap 1, the AR layer on top of existing POS.** POS vendors treat AR as someone else's add-on. A WebAR menu that syncs with Petpooja and Restroworks could be a partner rather than a replacement. Restaurants keep their POS and QR, and DineView supplies the "menu experience" plus 3D content.
- **Gap 2, nutrition and allergen data as a compliance and health product.** Chains with 10+ outlets must show kcal by law. A digital menu with per-dish calories, macros and allergens, maintained centrally, eases compliance and suits health-conscious urban diners. Bengaluru is a reasonable place to test this, given Zomato's healthy push includes it. Nutrition data accuracy, and who certifies it, is a liability question to resolve.
- **Gap 3, a premium and experience-led dine-in segment.** QR backlash and fine-dine upsell loss argue against pitching DineView as a waiter replacement. A better framing is that guests see the dish before ordering, while waiters keep upselling. This fits casual-premium and dessert, café and cocktail venues, where the Oxford dessert finding is most relevant.
- **Pricing benchmarks to position against:** Petpooja's QR+KDS bundle at ~₹20k–30k per outlet per year; an AR platform at ₹40k–60k per year (Petpooja estimate); capture at ₹1,500–4,000 per dish; ARmenu at £49 per dish; Kabaq at $99–199/month (2017).
- **Risks from history:** unproven ROI, content-production cost, menu churn, POS-bundled QR becoming a commodity, and small funding pools for this category in India (peAR <₹7 Cr; Thrive $2.5M then shutdown).
