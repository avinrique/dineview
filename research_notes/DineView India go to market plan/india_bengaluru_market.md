# India and Bengaluru restaurant / food-service market: fact base for DineView launch (as of late Oct 2026)

Research cut-off: 27 Oct 2026. Web search budget ran out partway through, so a few threads (listed under Gaps) could not be chased further. Figures are labelled with their year. "Search snippet" means the figure came from a search-result summary of that page and the full page was not opened.

## 1. Size and growth of India's food-service market (organised vs unorganised, dine-in vs delivery)

### Takeaway
There are two current baselines. NRAI's India Food Services Report (IFSR) 2024 puts the market at Rs 5,69,487 crore in FY24, rising to Rs 7,76,511 crore by FY28 (8.1% CAGR), with the organised share at 43.8% in FY24 rising to 52.9% by FY28. The newer NRAI + Redseer work (July 2026) puts the market at about US$90B, rising to US$150B by 2030/FY31, with organised players growing 17–18% a year and online delivery at only about 11% of the total in FY26. So about 89% of food-service spend is still offline: dine-in, takeaway and direct.

### Cited Findings
**NRAI IFSR 2024 (5th edition, released July 2024; data year FY24)**
- Market size Rs 5,69,487 crore in FY24, projected at Rs 7,76,511 crore by FY28, an overall CAGR of 8.1%. The organised segment's CAGR is 13.2% over FY24–28. India is projected to become the third-largest food-service market by 2028, overtaking Japan. — [Restaurant India, 10 Jul 2024](https://www.restaurantindia.in/article/india-to-be-the-3rd-largest-food-service-market-by-2028-overtaking-japan-nrai-ifsr-2024)
- Organised share is 43.8% now (FY24), projected at 52.9% by FY28. The organised segment is expected to overtake the unorganised one within 3–4 years and reach Rs 4.10 lakh crore. FY25 growth is expected at about 8%. Casual dining leads the organised segment, followed by QSR, cloud kitchens and cafés; the last three are named as the fastest-growing formats for 2024–28. — [CXO Digital Pulse summary of IFSR 2024](https://www.cxodigitalpulse.com/?p=19107)
- Unorganised share falls from 56.2% (FY24) to 47.1% (FY28). — [Goodreturns (search snippet)](https://www.goodreturns.in/news/india-food-services-sector-growth-forecast-fy28-011-1356351.html). This is consistent with 100% minus the organised share above.
- The sector shrank to about Rs 2 lakh crore in FY21 during COVID. — [India Retailing, 10 Jul 2024](https://www.indiaretailing.com/2024/07/10/food-services-sector-set-to-reach-rs-7-76-lakh-crore)
- Direct employment is 85.5 lakh (8.55 million) in 2024, expected to grow more than 20% by 2028. The sector's contribution to the exchequer is Rs 33,809 crore; the year and tax type are not specified. — [Restaurant India](https://www.restaurantindia.in/article/india-to-be-the-3rd-largest-food-service-market-by-2028-overtaking-japan-nrai-ifsr-2024)
- NRAI's GST ask: two slabs, 12% with input tax credit or 5% without. — [CXO Digital Pulse](https://www.cxodigitalpulse.com/?p=19107)
- Earlier edition, for trend: IFSR 2019 headline "Indian food service sector size Rs 4 lakh cr, employs 7.3 mn" (data year 2018-19). — [The Quint (headline only)](https://www.thequint.com/amp/story/news/hot-news/indian-food-service-sector-size-rs-4-lakh-cr-employs-7-3-mn)

**NRAI + Redseer, July 2026 (latest available)**
- Joint NRAI–Redseer report (reported 10 Jul 2026): about US$90B in FY24, rising to US$150B by FY31, a CAGR of about 10–11%. Online food delivery is about 11% of total food service today, rising to nearly 18% by FY31. — [Restaurant Times, 10 Jul 2026](https://www.restauranttimes.com/news/politics/india-food-services-market-fy31-report/)
- Redseer's own write-up of its July 2026 "State of India's Food Services Market" (published 18 Sep 2026; it does not mention NRAI):
  - Market was about $56B in 2020 and about $90B in 2025 (its FAQ says "2025-26"), projected at $150B by 2030.
  - Organised players are growing at a 17–18% CAGR; the unorganised segment at 3–4%.
  - Organised share was 35–40% in 2020 and is 45–50% now.
  - Source: [Redseer digest](https://redseer.com/digests/india-food-services-market-growth-profitability-2030/)
- Redseer, same page: there are about 1,000–1,500 organised food-service companies. 89–90% earn under Rs 50 crore revenue, 7–8% earn Rs 50–500 crore, and about 2% earn over Rs 500 crore. — [Redseer digest](https://redseer.com/digests/india-food-services-market-growth-profitability-2030/)
- Redseer, same page, on online versus offline:
  - Online delivery's share of food services was about 4% in FY21 and about 11% in FY26, projected at 18% by FY31.
  - Online is growing at a 20–22% CAGR; offline at 8–10%.
  - New-age brands make about 90% of revenue online. Legacy chains make about 50% (generally 35–50%).
  - Monthly transacting food-delivery users rose from about 1 crore (FY21) to about 3 crore (FY26).
  - Source: [Redseer digest](https://redseer.com/digests/india-food-services-market-growth-profitability-2030/)
- Redseer, same page, on categories:
  - Out-of-home coffee was about $3.2B in 2025, projected at $4–4.5B by 2030. Premium coffee (above Rs 200 a cup) is growing 20–25% a year.
  - Out-of-home tea was about $1.8B in 2025, projected at $3.5–4B by 2030.
  - Top premium beverage brands reach same-store sales growth of 25%; laggards are at 2–3%.
  - Source: [Redseer digest](https://redseer.com/digests/india-food-services-market-growth-profitability-2030/)
- Conflict: Restaurant Times dates the US$90B base to FY24, while Redseer dates it to 2025 / 2025-26. The forecast horizon also differs (FY31 vs 2030). — [Restaurant Times](https://www.restauranttimes.com/news/politics/india-food-services-market-fy31-report/) vs [Redseer](https://redseer.com/digests/india-food-services-market-growth-profitability-2030/)
- Other estimate: the Kearney–Swiggy "How India Eats" report projects the market to cross US$125B by 2030. — [Outlook Business](https://www.outlookbusiness.com/news/indian-food-service-market-projected-to-cross-usd-125-bn-by-2030-report). A search snippet attributes a 2025 base of about US$78B to the same report; not verified in the full text.

**Dine-in scale signals from the aggregators (FY26 / FY27)**
- Swiggy Dineout, Q4 FY26 (Jan–Mar 2026):
  - GOV was Rs 1,245 crore, up 43% YoY.
  - Average monthly active Dineout restaurants rose from 39k (Q4 FY25) to 53k (Q4 FY26). The letter text says "over 52k ... growing at 36% YoY".

- Eternal's District (going-out business: dining, movies, events), Q1 FY26: annualised net order value (NOV) of Rs 8,000 crore. It had about 2 million monthly transacting customers making about 2 transactions a month, at a net AOV of Rs 1,700+. Management sees a possible US$3B topline with $150M Adjusted EBITDA in about 5 years. — [Storyboard18, 21 Jul 2025](https://www.storyboard18.com/digital/district-now-accounts-for-20-of-eternals-business-deepinder-goyal-75719.htm)
- District, Q1 FY27 (Apr–Jun 2026):
  - NOV grew 60% YoY to Rs 3,218 crore.
  - Revenue grew 54% to Rs 318 crore.
  - The segment is loss-making, at about −Rs 61–62 crore EBITDA.
  - Eternal stopped reporting GOV and now reports NOV.
  - Sources: [Upstox](https://upstox.com/news/market-news/earnings/eternal-q1-results-eternal-profit-surges-268-yo-y-to-92-crore-revenue-climbs-182/article-197377/); [IndiaInfoline](https://www.indiainfoline.com/news/earnings/eternal-q1-fy27-results-net-profit-jumps-3-7x-to-92-crore-as-blinkit-becomes-biggest-revenue-driver)

### Inferences
- If online delivery is about 11% of food service in FY26 (Redseer), then about 89% of spend (roughly US$80B) is offline: dine-in, takeaway and unorganised. Even within the organised segment, dine-in remains the larger channel for legacy and casual-dining chains, which generate only about 35–50% of revenue online. DineView's table-side product targets that offline majority.
- Organised growth (13–18% CAGR, depending on source) is 3–5 times unorganised growth. Organised outlets (chains and organised standalones) are the realistic SaaS buyers, and their share is passing 50% around FY27–28.
- Aggregator dine-in platforms (Swiggy Dineout with 53k active restaurants, Eternal's District) are growing 40–60% YoY. They are training premium diners to discover and pay for dine-in through apps, which is both a tailwind (habit) and a competitive threat (they own the guest relationship).

### Gaps
- I could not obtain the IFSR 2024 PDF itself, so the exact dine-in / takeaway / delivery split and the chain vs standalone split from NRAI are not confirmed.
- No NRAI-branded "IFSR 2026" was found. The July 2026 NRAI + Redseer report appears to be the latest, and its INR figures and organised share were not available in the sources reached.
- The 2025 base of the Kearney–Swiggy report (US$78B) was not verified.

## 2. Bengaluru outlet counts, format mix, aggregator listings and premium-dining neighbourhoods

### Takeaway
No official census of Bengaluru restaurants exists in the sources found. The best current proxy comes from the Bengaluru hotel associations, as reported in August 2026: about 34,000 "hotels and restaurants" in the city, nearly 20,000 of them on Swiggy/Zomato. A broader, unsourced trade-press figure is 110,000+ "foodservice outlets", which likely includes unorganised, bakery and street formats. Bengaluru is clearly India's top dine-out and F&B-leasing city, and its premium dining clusters around Indiranagar, Koramangala, the MG Road / Church Street / UB City CBD, and Whitefield.

### Cited Findings
**Total counts**
- About 34,000 hotels and restaurants in Bengaluru, of which "nearly 20,000" are listed on food-delivery platforms. These are association figures reported during the August 2026 Swiggy/Zomato boycott threat; industry bodies said "more than 20,000" restaurants could go offline. — [South First, 5 Aug 2026](https://thesouthfirst.com/karnataka/bengaluru-eateries-threaten-to-boycott-swiggy-zomato-over-unfair-business-practices/)
- Bengaluru "ranks among India's top three foodservice markets after Mumbai and Delhi-NCR, with over 110,000 outlets", with industry growth estimated at 8–13% a year. No source is given. — [Franchise India, 20 Jan 2026](https://www.franchiseindia.com/index.php/insights/en/news/french-bagel-brand-bagelstein-debuts-in-bengaluru-opens-third-outlet-in-india.58233)
- Older NRAI city report (edition and year not confirmed; likely the 2016–2019 era):
  - Bengaluru's organised food-service market (chain plus organised standalone) was Rs 7,870 crore, 8% of India's organised market.
  - The unorganised market was Rs 3,870 crore, about 33% share.
  - Bengaluru was the "second fastest growing major metropolis" and the "fourth fastest growing Foodservice market".
  - Source: [India Retailing (search snippet; page body did not render)](https://www.indiaretailing.com/?p=79792)

### Inferences
- A workable Bengaluru addressable-market ladder: about 34,000 eateries (association count), of which about 20,000 are on delivery apps. Of those, the dine-in-relevant, premium and casual-dining subset is probably low single-digit thousands. That subset is my estimate, not sourced. It rests on Swiggy Dineout having 53k active restaurants nationally, Bengaluru being its #1 dine-in city, and premium/casual-dining formats being a minority of listings.
- Bengaluru's mix is unusually rich in breweries and large-format pubs: 70–80+ microbreweries, 40% of new pubs and breweries per JLL, and #1 for group and late-night bookings. These are high-ticket, menu-heavy, large-table venues where table ordering, upselling and visual menus pay back fastest. They are a natural first ICP alongside premium casual dining in Indiranagar, Koramangala, CBD (MG Road / Church Street / UB City / Brigade Road) and Whitefield/Sarjapur (ORR tech corridor).
- In Bengaluru usage, "hotel" commonly means a restaurant or eatery (darshinis, "hotels" such as Vidyarthi Bhavan), so the association's 34,000 "hotels and restaurants" is mostly restaurants, not lodging.

### Gaps

- No neighbourhood-level restaurant counts or density data were found. CBRE, JLL and Knight Frank give leasing shares, not counts. A Curlytales headline says Bangalore has 4 of the top 10 high streets with rents of "₹23,928 and ₹9,500" per sq ft, but the units and period were unclear, so it is not used.
- The IFSR 2024 Bengaluru city chapter, if one exists, was not found. The Mumbai chapter is cited in section 3.

## 3. Average order values / cost for two, dine-in frequency, and Bengaluru vs other metros

### Takeaway
NRAI says urban Indians eat out about 8 times a month (FY24, up from 6.6 in 2018-19). For premium dine-in, the aggregator data points to tickets in the Rs 1,700–3,200 range, versus about Rs 400–430 for a delivery order. Bengaluru is India's #1 city on Swiggy Dineout by diners seated and group bookings, and it leads F&B leasing. In NRAI market value it ranks behind Mumbai and Delhi-NCR.

### Inferences
- A premium Bengaluru table plausibly spends about Rs 1,500–3,000 for two (Swiggy listings; District net AOV of Rs 1,700+), against a sub-Rs 450 delivery order. A per-table upsell lift of even 5–10% is therefore worth Rs 75–300 a table, which is the key ROI lever for a per-outlet SaaS fee.
- Bengaluru is the best Indian city to launch premium dine-in tech on behaviour (#1 diners and group bookings, Premium Dining growth above 120%, pub density), even though Mumbai is larger in organised market value.

### Gaps
- No survey-based average "cost for two" for Bengaluru casual vs premium dining was found from Zomato, Swiggy or NRAI.

- Comparable 2024–26 city figures for Delhi NCR, Hyderabad, Pune and Chennai (market value, outlets) were not found, beyond Swiggy's diner counts.
- No Bengaluru-specific eating-out frequency was found.

## 4. Digital adoption in Indian restaurants (QR menus/ordering, POS, UPI, software)

### Takeaway
Digital payments are effectively universal: UPI hit 228.5 billion transactions in 2025, and restaurants are consistently among the top in-store UPI categories. POS adoption is widespread among organised outlets but fragmented, with no single vendor above about one-third even in a dense metro. Hard, current data on QR-menu or QR-ordering adoption does not exist publicly; the most-quoted figure ("about one-third use QR ordering") is a 2021 vendor estimate.

### Cited Findings
- **QR ordering:**
  - "Nearly one-third of restaurants in India are actively using QR code ordering technology since the Covid-19 hit" (POSist PRIME report, cited Mar 2021). ## 6. Regulation: FSSAI menu display (calories, nutrition, allergens, veg/non-veg) and Karnataka rules

### Takeaway
Menu display has been mandatory since 1 January 2022 (with calorie sampling held off until 30 June 2022) for food-service establishments that hold a central FSSAI licence or have outlets at 10 or more locations. Under the FSS (Labelling and Display) Regulations 2020, these establishments must show:
- calories (kcal per serving plus serving size) and a 2,000 kcal reference line
- allergens and the veg/non-veg logo, on the menu card or board
- nutrition information on request (booklet, handout or website)

Delivery apps must also publish this data (from 1 July 2022). FSSAI has enforced it: about 15–16 licences were suspended in 2022, and an April 2024 clarification confirmed the rule while allowing flexible display formats. No repeal or deadline change was found as of 2026. Karnataka adds hygiene and veg/non-veg-separation advisories (August 2026) and Kannada-signboard rules, but no menu-language rule was found.

### Cited Findings
- **Origin and timeline:**
  - Draft published 8 Oct 2018.
  - Notified Aug 2020 (the article says 21 Aug; the gazette file name says 26 Aug 2020) as Rule 2.4.6 "Display of information in food service establishments" under the FSS (Packaging and Labelling) Regulations 2011.
  - Voluntary until 1 Jan 2022, then mandatory.
  - Source: [S.S. Rana & Co., 8 Sep 2020](https://ssrana.in/?p=14156)
- **Move into the 2020 Regulations:** the restaurant-chain provisions were "later subsumed into the newly enacted Food Safety and Standards (Labelling and Display) Regulations, 2020". Applicability is to chains "operating in multiple states or having outlets at 10 or more locations", from 1 Jan 2022. — [LiveLaw column, Jun 2021](https://www.livelaw.in/amp/columns/food-safety-and-standards-authority-of-india-fssai-food-safety-and-standards-act-2006-act-176134)
- **Who must comply:** food-service establishments "having central license or outlets at ten or more locations". — [S.S. Rana](https://ssrana.in/?p=14156)
  - Practitioner reading: a central licence alone triggers the rule, so a small chain operating across two or more states, which must hold a central licence, can be covered below 10 outlets. This is a vendor interpretation. — [Petpooja blog (search snippet)](https://blog.petpooja.com/industry-business-guides/multi-outlet-menu-management-update-every-outlet/)
- **What must be displayed:**
  - Calorific value in kcal per serving plus serving size, against items on menu cards, boards or booklets.
  - The statement "An average active adult requires 2,000 kcal energy per day, however, calorie needs may vary".
  - Allergens: gluten-containing cereals, crustaceans, milk, eggs, fish, groundnuts and tree nuts, soybeans, and sulphite at 10 mg/kg or more. Symbols are allowed.
  - The veg/non-veg logo on the menu card.
  - Nutrition and organic information where applicable.
  - A 25% tolerance applies to nutrition declarations.
  - Source: [S.S. Rana](https://ssrana.in/?p=14156)
  - A June 2021 column also mentions an MSG health warning ("not suitable for infants and pregnant women"). — [LiveLaw](https://www.livelaw.in/amp/columns/food-safety-and-standards-authority-of-india-fssai-food-safety-and-standards-act-2006-act-176134)
- **Exemptions:**
  - event caterers
  - premises operating under 60 days a year
  - free self-service condiments not on the menu
  - special-order or modified meals
  - Source: [S.S. Rana](https://ssrana.in/?p=14156)
- **Transition:** FSSAI directed that menu items not be sampled to verify calorie/nutrition declarations until 30 Jun 2022. One compliance source describes the rule as "effective on 1st July 2022" under Chapter III of the 2020 Regulations. — [Lexplosion](https://lexplosion.in/importance-of-food-menu-labelling-by-food-business-operators/)
- **E-commerce:** e-commerce FBOs must obtain the information from restaurants and display it. In June 2022 FSSAI told Swiggy, Zomato and others to ensure compliance from 1 Jul 2022, including in mobile apps. — [Agron Food Processing, 8 Sep 2022](https://agronfoodprocessing.com/fssai-suspended-15-food-business-operators-licences-for-not-displaying-nutritional-value/)
- **Enforcement (2022):** about 15 (the article body says 16) FBO licences were suspended for not displaying nutritional value, and about 70 FBOs sought time. FSSAI's approach was self-compliance first, then suspension. — [Agron Food Processing, 8 Sep 2022](https://agronfoodprocessing.com/fssai-suspended-15-food-business-operators-licences-for-not-displaying-nutritional-value/)
- **Clarification of 12 April 2024** (issued after licensing authorities misinterpreted the rule, leading to suspensions in some cases):
  - Food-service establishments have "discretion / flexibility to select any one method".
  - Calories go on menu cards, boards or booklets.
  - Allergens and the veg/non-veg logo go on menu cards or boards.
  - Nutritional information goes in booklets, handouts or the website.
  - The same letter re-directed e-commerce aggregators to obtain and display the information.
  - Sources: [Lexplosion, 2 May 2024](https://lexplosion.in/fssai-re-iterates-requirement-for-e-commerce-food-aggregators-to-obtain-nutritional-information-from-food-business-operators-and-display-on-their-platforms/); [FoodSafetyWorks/MyFSSAI listing (search snippet)](https://foodsafetyworks.com/fssai_updates/fssai-clarification-with-regard-to-the-display-of-information-by-food-service-establishments)
- **Penalty exposure:** a 2021 legal column notes misbranding fines of up to Rs 3 lakh (Section 52, FSS Act 2006). It says allergen misinformation could attract Section 59 penalties of up to life imprisonment and Rs 10 lakh; that is the author's view. — [LiveLaw](https://www.livelaw.in/amp/columns/food-safety-and-standards-authority-of-india-fssai-food-safety-and-standards-act-2006-act-176134)
- **Conflicting 2026 web claims:** vendor blogs say FSSAI "recommends calorie disclosure ... moving toward mandatory labelling", and one claims a 25+ outlet threshold. Neither cites any FSSAI notice. — [DineCard blog (Jul 2026)](https://www.dinecard.in/blog/digital-menu-calories-nutritional-information-display-fssai). These contradict the legal sources above; I found no FSSAI notice rescinding or changing the 10-outlet / central-licence rule.
- **Karnataka FDA advisories, 11 Aug 2026** (hotels, dhabas and food-service establishments) **and 20 Aug 2026** (all FBOs):
  - display the licence prominently
  - hold hot food above 60°C and cold food below 5°C
  - use separate boards, knives and utensils for veg and non-veg
  - no unsafe artificial colours
  - FoSTaC-trained food safety supervisor on site
  - medical fitness records for food handlers
  - 15 days to rectify deficiencies, then re-inspection and FSS Act action
  - Source: [Fox Mandal](https://foxmandal.in/news/karnatakas-food-safety-drive-what-the-august-2026-advisories-mean-for-food-businesses/)
- **Karnataka artificial colours (2024):** the state banned artificial colours in kebabs, fish dishes and gobi manchurian, according to news reports. Penalty claims (7 years to life, Rs 10 lakh) were not verified against the notification. — [Deccan Herald (search snippet)](https://www.deccanherald.com/amp/story/india%2Fkarnataka%2Fkarnataka-health-minister-asks-people-not-to-be-fooled-by-attractive-colourful-dishes-3665086)
- **Kannada signboards:** the Kannada Language Comprehensive Development (Amendment) Act, 2024 requires name boards of establishments, hotels included, to be 60% Kannada. — [Simpliance](https://simpliance.in/India/LEI/govt_notification/karnataka/the-kannada-language-comprehensive-development-amendment-act-2024-2026). The Karnataka High Court gave an interim order restraining BBMP from coercive action against some petitioners (date not confirmed). — [The News Minute](https://www.thenewsminute.com/karnataka/signboards-kannada-hc-asks-bbmp-not-take-coercive-action-against-establishments-114710)

### Inferences
- **Sales wedge:** the obligated buyers are chains with 10+ outlets nationally, or any operator holding a central licence (multi-state operators, large-turnover operators). In Bengaluru that means national and regional QSR, café and casual-dining chains and multi-city brewery/pub groups, not single-outlet premium standalones. For standalones, nutrition, allergen and veg info is a voluntary differentiator.
- DineView's per-dish calories, macros, allergens and veg/non-veg display maps directly onto the April 2024 permitted methods. Its nutrition detail page could serve as the "website" route for nutritional information. A calorie and allergen database maintained per dish also feeds the e-commerce (Swiggy/Zomato) display duty, which chains must comply with anyway.
- The Karnataka FDA's August 2026 hygiene drive raises compliance attention among Bengaluru operators right now, which is useful timing for a compliance-led pitch. Note that the advisories themselves do not cover menus.

### Gaps
- I could not open the official FSSAI gazette text or the 2022 Guidance Note PDF; the PDF was scanned images. The exact regulation number in the 2020 Regulations (sources cite "Regulation 9" or "Chapter III") is not verified.
- Whether a QR / digital menu alone (with no printed card) satisfies "menu cards or boards" is not explicitly addressed in any source found. This needs legal confirmation.
